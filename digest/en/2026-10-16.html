Budgets, truncation, and transfer

Frontier pretraining · Muon budget comparisons · STOPD · DPO close reading

Three focal questions: preserving repetition when scaling experiments, translating Muon’s data efficiency into time efficiency, and identifying which gradient quantity random truncation preserves. Briefs cover additional manuscripts and revisions; the classic derives DPO’s closed-form policy and preference loss.

MAI-Base-2: scale unique data along with the model

Frontier Pre-training at Microsoft AI ↗
Microsoft AI
2026-10-15 · Official technical report/blog

Transferring a short scaling run to long training requires preserving repetition. Let NNN be active parameters, DDD training tokens and UUU unique tokens. The report preserves TPP and each source’s repetition rate. A one-source rewrite is: [1]

With steps sss, learning rate η\etaη and weight decay λ\lambdaλ, a quadratic fit in log-hyperparameter coordinates gives these empirical optima: [1]

The final model has 2.8T total/80B active parameters and trains on 50T tokens. Momentum resharding, batched all-to-all and specialized Newton–Schulz kernels put the Muon optimizer step at 2.1% of the average iteration (165 ms). [1]

Evidence boundary

The reported 4.2–12.3× is FLOPs efficiency against DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base under four held-out BPB fits. Those fits include the final-model point: neither unseen-scale prediction nor an isolated Muon wall-clock gain. [1]

Muon optimality: radial laws versus minimax geometry

When Is Muon Optimal? Radial Fragility and Minimax Geometry ↗
Ao Xu
First posted 2026-10-15 05:13 EDT · v1 · Overlap-window addition

For convex nondecreasing radial objectives, exact polar optimality is fragile under a fixed atomless radial law, while worst-case optimization over radial laws can recover the polar direction. Full-column-rank and other assumptions matter; this is not a general nonconvex training guarantee. [4]
