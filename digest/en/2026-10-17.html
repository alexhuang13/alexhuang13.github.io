What to prune, what to preserve

Data pruning · Elastic models · Post-training blogs · Chinchilla close reading

Two focal papers study pruning training examples and model units; technical blogs add synthetic-data and post-training details. Papers dated October 14–15 are overlap-window additions, not today’s releases. The Chinchilla classic derives optimal allocation conditional on a credible empirical loss surface.

2N: not simply the hardest examples

Hard Way, The Only Way: Provable Merits of Training on Hardest Examples ↗
Elvis Dohmatob
First posted 2026-10-15 00:00 EDT · v1 · Overlap-window addition

Sorscher et al. (2022) studied pruning-induced changes to learning curves. [3] Here the setting is Gaussian linear classification: dimension NNN, sample count PPP, perfectly aligned generator/test/pruner directions, and ridgeless squared loss. [1]

Proportional high-dimensional limit, then small phi; A selects margins
ϕ=NP,T=∣G∣, G∼N(0,1),p=Pr⁡(T∈A),μk=E[Tk∣T∈A],ν=Var⁡(T∣T∈A)\phi=\frac NP,\quad T=|G|,\ G\sim\mathcal N(0,1),\quad p=\Pr(T\in A),\quad \mu_k=\mathbb E[T^k\mid T\in A],\quad\nu=\operatorname{Var}(T\mid T\in A)ϕ=PN​,T=∣G∣, G∼N(0,1),p=Pr(T∈A),μk​=E[Tk∣T∈A],ν=Var(T∣T∈A)

The paper reduces classification error to an angle; retention must exceed the dimension/sample ratio. [1]

Evidence boundary

The optimal set is a near-boundary thin band excluding the origin, not [0,c][0,c][0,c]. A perfect pruner is not free, and this is not an LLM theorem. General convex-loss analysis uses replicas. We focus on the solvable case; inconsistent bandwidth notation in the manuscript is avoided by using the density bound. [1]

Freeze weights, learn one cross-budget ranking

Your Transformer Is Secretly Elastic ↗
Walter Simoncini · Dheeraj Varghese · Gertjan J. Burghouts · Cees G. M. Snoek
First posted 2026-10-14 13:58 EDT · v1 · Overlap-window addition

Fixed-target pruning typically repeats work for each budget. Here only global MLP-neuron/head scores zzz are learned; backbone θ\thetaθ stays frozen. Let bbb denote removed FLOPs and κb\kappa_bκb​ the cost-weighted ranking threshold. [2]

Compact method rewrite; sg stops threshold gradients; hard masks at deployment
Mb(z)=σ ⁣(z−sg⁡(κb(z))τ),min⁡z1∣B∣∑b∈BEDℓ(fθ,Mb(z)(x),y)M_b(z)=\sigma\!\left(\frac{z-\operatorname{sg}(\kappa_b(z))}{\tau}\right),\qquad \min_z\frac1{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}\ell(f_{\theta,M_b(z)}(x),y)Mb​(z)=σ(τz−sg(κb​(z))​),zmin​∣B∣1​b∈B∑​ED​ℓ(fθ,Mb​(z)​(x),y)

Fourteen targets (5%–70%), 300 Adam calibration steps; ViT-B uses batch 32 and 2,000 unique images by default. One ranking yields nested subnetworks, not optimization-free adaptation. [2]

Evidence boundary

Evidence mainly supports vision/bidirectional encoders, not autoregressive language generation. Calibration cost and inference FLOPs are distinct budget axes. [2]

AI4Bharat · NVIDIA
2026-10-16 · Technical blog; blog date is not model release date

Translate, judge, repair, transliterate: 75/100 is a rubric threshold, not accuracy. Beam: language adherence, not reasoning accuracy

Welcome Beam: a 501B open model you can own, customize, and scale ↗
Ben Burtenshaw · Merve Noyan · Pedro Cuenca
2026-10-16 · Technical blog; blog date is not model release date

One epoch of Multilingual-Thinking LoRA SFT changes requested-language adherence from 3/16 to 16/16 held-out prompts: tiny sample, no reasoning-correctness evaluation. Separately, the GRPO recipe uses 32 prompts × 8 responses, group advantages and truncated importance sampling, without KL/entropy penalties. A runnable recipe is not demonstrated downstream improvement. [5]

Muse Spark: attention-only LoRA on 816B

Welcome Muse Spark Open: open-weight frontier multimodal mixture-of-experts model from Meta ↗
Hugging Face team
2026-10-15 · Technical blog; blog date is not model release date

Experts remain frozen; LoRA targets four attention projections. The reported run uses 64 H100s, 500 steps, length 2,048 and packing, taking about two hours. Training token accuracy rises 72%→84% and loss falls 1.26→0.57; these are training metrics, not held-out capability gains. [6]

Apertus: post-training can erode long-context ability

Apertus 1.5 Release Notes ↗
Hanna Yukhymenko · Oleg Lavrovsky · Y Metz
2026-10-15 · Technical blog; blog date is not model release date

Weights were released July 24; this is a new technical write-up. Five stages: SFT → long-context SFT → RL → DPO → SDPO. Reasoning traces are added only where non-thinking fails; tool trajectories require execution verification. Long-context SFT restores ability eroded by short-context SFT; no isolated causal ablation is established here. [7]

Chinchilla: from an empirical surface to optimal allocation

Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models ↗
Jordan Hoffmann · Sebastian Borgeaud · Arthur Mensch et al.
Historical classic: first posted/read v1, 2022-03-29; NeurIPS 2022
NeurIPS 2022 · historical scaling-laws landmark ↗

Kaplan et al. (2020) favored larger models and less data under fixed compute. [32] Chinchilla re-estimates allocation with over 400 runs: the goal is not convergence, but maximal loss reduction per finite FLOPs budget. [30][31]

Let NNN be parameters, DDD training tokens and CCC training FLOPs. This is an empirical model with positive coefficients, not a neural-network theorem. [30]

Original parametric fit; E is the irreducible term
L^(N,D)=E+AN−α+BD−β,C≃6ND\widehat L(N,D)=E+A N^{-\alpha}+B D^{-\beta},\qquad C\simeq6NDL(N,D)=E+AN−α+BD−β,C≃6ND

Substitution of the budget constraint balances marginal gains, not the two loss terms themselves:

Algebra conditional on the empirical model
∂∂log⁡N[AN−α+B(C6N)−β]=0⟺αAN−α=βBD−β\frac{\partial}{\partial\log N}\left[A N^{-\alpha}+B\left(\frac C{6N}\right)^{-\beta}\right]=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\alpha A N^{-\alpha}=\beta B D^{-\beta}∂logN∂​[AN−α+B(6NC​)−β]=0⟺αAN−α=βBD−β
Original allocation; continuous N,D approximation
G=(αAβB)1/(α+β),N⋆=G(C/6)β/(α+β),D⋆=G−1(C/6)α/(α+β)G=\left(\frac{\alpha A}{\beta B}\right)^{1/(\alpha+\beta)},\qquad N_\star=G(C/6)^{\beta/(\alpha+\beta)},\qquad D_\star=G^{-1}(C/6)^{\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)}G=(βBαA​)1/(α+β),N⋆​=G(C/6)β/(α+β),D⋆​=G−1(C/6)α/(α+β)
Our direct consequence: a constant token/parameter ratio additionally requires alpha=beta
D⋆N⋆=G−2(C/6)(α−β)/(α+β)\frac{D_\star}{N_\star}=G^{-2}(C/6)^{(\alpha-\beta)/(\alpha+\beta)}N⋆​D⋆​​=G−2(C/6)(α−β)/(α+β)
Evidence boundary

“About 20 tokens per parameter” is not universal. Schedule length must match training duration; intermediate points on a long schedule are not optimized short runs. Data mixture, repetition and inference cost can change applicability. [30]
