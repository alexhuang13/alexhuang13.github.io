Budgets Shape Geometry; States Compress History

BMuon · Small-scale extrapolation · Async RL · Linear Transformers

Channel budgets, small-model extrapolation, and gradient bias from stale rollouts: seven newly collected research items and technical articles, plus two version records whose substantive changes remain unverified. Original dates are retained; none is presented as first published today. The classic asks how linear attention compresses a growing history into a fixed-size state.

PONDER: spend extra compute on which positions to revise

PONDER: Learning to Generate in the Right Order ↗
Julius Erbach · Dominik Narnhofer · Bernt Schiele · Jan Eric Lenssen · Konrad Schindler
First posted October 16, 2026, 10:29 EDT · v1

Freeze the generator and train a fill/remove position policy. N empty positions and R removals require N+2R position decisions, not that many backbone calls. For a fixed Sudoku policy trained at R=9, hard accuracy rises from 56.60% at R=0 to 96.68% at R=9 and 98.61% at R=128; evaluation uses 30,000 held-out boards balanced across three difficulty levels. This is not a compute-matched comparison.[4]

Linear Transformers: turning history into state

Transformers are RNNs: Fast Autoregressive Transformers with Linear Attention ↗

The Transformer (2017) lets each query retrieve the entire history, with a growing cache. This ICML 2020 work uses finite-dimensional kernel features to compress history into a matrix state: it changes the attention kernel rather than obtaining exact softmax at no cost.[10][11]

Evidence boundary

A finite-m ELU kernel is not the exponential softmax kernel. Fixed state saves computation while compressing history into the same statistics; these identities do not guarantee lossless arbitrary long-range retrieval.

Discussion question

If two distinct histories produce identical (Sₜ,zₜ) but the next query requires different answers, this layer cannot distinguish them. Can we construct a minimal collision and identify what additional state is needed?
