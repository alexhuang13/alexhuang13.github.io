Polar Switch: optimize the circuit before approximating polar

Polar Switch: Fast Muon Orthogonalization with Polynomial Circuits ↗
Zhehang Du · Weijie Su
First posted 2026-10-19 13:06 EDT · v1

Muon's cost depends on reusing matrix products, not only polynomial degree. For normalized X, Polar Switch searches polynomial circuits with limited buffers; the corresponding singular-value map is an odd polynomial. [1]

Spectral map in exact arithmetic; not an elementwise rule for arbitrary noncommuting buffers
X=UΣV⊤,X←h(XX⊤)X=U[Σh(Σ2)]V⊤,p(σ)=σh(σ2)X=U\Sigma V^\top,\qquad X\leftarrow h(XX^\top)X=U[\Sigma h(\Sigma^2)]V^\top,\qquad p(\sigma)=\sigma h(\sigma^2)X=UΣV⊤,X←h(XX⊤)X=U[Σh(Σ2)]V⊤,p(σ)=σh(σ2)
Restricted circuit: k matrix products; both coefficients and buffer connections are searched
Bw←aBiBj+bBr+cI,deg⁡p≤2k+1+1B_w\leftarrow aB_iB_j+bB_r+cI,\qquad \deg p\le 2^{k+1}+1Bw​←aBi​Bj​+bBr​+cI,degp≤2k+1+1
Evidence boundary

Square-matrix kernel time falls 15.5–18.7% versus Polar Express, not end-to-end training time. Long Qwen-0.6B / Gemma-1B runs use one seed each; the rounding model omits some additive errors. [1]

Loop depth versus backward depth: truncation need not save compute

Truncate or Not? A Scaling Study of Looped Transformers with Truncated Backpropagation ↗
Xuyuan Xiong · Siyu Chen · Zihan Shao · Yue Su · Weiyang Jin · Ping Luo · Zhuoran Yang · Xihui Liu
First posted 2026-10-19 04:52 EDT · v1

Let T be forward loops and B≤T backward loops. Across 592 runs with 58–919M non-embedding parameters, repeated computation is mapped to effective parameters; N_once / N_rec count non-recurrent / recurrent parameters. [2]

Empirical fit; D counts training tokens, not a universal scaling theorem
L=E+[A(Neff/N0)−α+H(D/D0)−β]κ,Neff=Nonce+g(T,B)NrecL=E+\left[A(N_{\rm eff}/N_0)^{-\alpha}+H(D/D_0)^{-\beta}\right]^\kappa,\quad N_{\rm eff}=N_{\rm once}+g(T,B)N_{\rm rec}L=E+[A(Neff​/N0​)−α+H(D/D0​)−β]κ,Neff​=Nonce​+g(T,B)Nrec​
Fitted p=0.23677, q=1.02181; C approximates leading FLOPs
g(T,B)=exp⁡{c(1−T−pB−q)},C≈6D[Nonce+T+2B3Nrec]g(T,B)=\exp\{c(1-T^{-p}B^{-q})\},\qquad C\approx6D\left[N_{\rm once}+\frac{T+2B}{3}N_{\rm rec}\right]g(T,B)=exp{c(1−T−pB−q)},C≈6D[Nonce​+3T+2B​Nrec​]
Within the feasible continuous relaxation: when T and B are both free, the fitted model favors full backpropagation
q>2p⟹arg⁡max⁡1≤B≤T, T+2B=Sg(T,B)=(S/3,S/3)q>2p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \arg\max_{1\le B\le T,\ T+2B=S}g(T,B)=(S/3,S/3)q>2p⟹arg1≤B≤T, T+2B=Smax​g(T,B)=(S/3,S/3)
Evidence boundary

Fixing T differs from optimizing both T and B. If parameter count is also free, this fit favors non-looped models within the studied budget range. Results depend on bounded g, architecture and training range. [2]

More data makes the validation distribution matter

On Data Scaling in Tabular ML: More Data, Better Generalization? ↗
Ivan Rubachev · Nikita Lazarev · Artem Babenko
First posted 2026-10-19 13:40 EDT · v1

Eight real tabular tasks scale to ten million samples, evaluated under random splits and temporal/group shifts. Error separates into a decreasing term and a floor for dataset d and method m: [3]

E is RMSE or 1−AP; shared exponents are shared within a dataset only
Ed,m(N)=ad,m(N/104)−b+cd,m,b=bd or bd,m\mathcal E_{d,m}(N)=a_{d,m}(N/10^4)^{-b}+c_{d,m},\qquad b=b_d\ \text{or}\ b_{d,m}Ed,m​(N)=ad,m​(N/104)−b+cd,m​,b=bd​ or bd,m​

Tuning on in-distribution validation can worsen out-of-distribution performance as data grows. Deployment-representative validation mitigates this in MLP experiments; adding OOD examples to PFN context does not always fix it. [3]

Evidence boundary

High fitted R² does not establish reliable extrapolation, nor a universal exponent across tasks. The study does not prove that more data necessarily harms generalization.

Score-SDE: same marginals, different paths
