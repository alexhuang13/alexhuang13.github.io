Exact polar: the error spectrum controls progress

A Quadratic Lens on Muon's Polar Update: Benefits, Pitfalls, and the Role of Newton-Schulz ↗
Egor Shulgin · Sam Laing · Antonio Orvieto · Peter Richtárik
First posted 2026-10-21 14:01 EDT · v1; October 23 listing

Exact line search compares directions on matrix quadratics without learning-rate scaling effects. The analyzed PolarGD has no momentum.[1]

If EE^T=cI, p(E)=1 gives one-step convergence. Even with H=I, nearly rank-deficient errors can make PolarGD much slower than GD. Finite NS amplifies tiny singular values approximately linearly instead of lifting them directly to one.[1]

Evidence boundary

Quadratic line-search results do not directly cover nonconvex stochastic training with momentum. Greedy per-step progress need not produce the best trajectory.[1]

R-squared-D: remask-denoise as an inference-scaling axis

Random Remasking Scales Masked Diffusion Inference ↗
Zhaoyi Zhou · Frank Wu · Andrea Zanette
First posted October 22, 01:15 EDT · v1

A Markov chain over clean sequences motivates inference-only R-squared-D; TCT additionally trains on trajectory errors. Logical tasks show near-log-linear scaling, over 97% on hard Sudoku and over 99% on 3-SAT. These results cannot all be attributed to training-free R-squared-D alone.[5]

Replace unverifiable subset optimality with full-data faithfulness

Ahmed Mehdi Inane · Mahdi Haghifam · Gintare Karolina Dziugaite · Ioannis Mitliagkas

Width-depth training limits: clipping changes the convergence mode

Width and Depth Limits Commute During Training ↗
Ruijia Zhang · Soufiane Hayou
First posted October 22, 00:56 EDT · v1

For linear ResNets with 1/sqrt(depth) Depth-muP scaling and muP width parameterization, any fixed clipped-GD horizon has the same L2 limit along all joint growth paths. Without clipping, only convergence in probability is established; nonlinear networks and long horizons are outside this result.[8]

Predictable performance is suboptimal, and what it means for model routing ↗
Guanhua Zhang · Moritz Hardt
First posted October 22, 11:27 EDT · v1

A predictor's advantage over chosen-answer confidence transfers exactly to log-loss improvement through recalibration. Across 16 models and four benchmarks, predictor-based prediction and cascading gains shrink after finetuning. Predictor capacity must be counted in the comparison.[11]

ThinkingBox: repeatable success rises while coverage slips

Turning Database Grading into RL Rewards: Post-Training Agents with ThinkingBox ↗
Tuhin Kundu · Microsoft and Hugging Face collaborators
Blog published October 22

After 50 GRPO steps on a separate training pool, Qwen3.8-27B is tested 20 times on each of 507 tasks. Pass@1 rises 51.70% to 60.78%; observed 20/20 tasks rise 38 to 107, but pass@20 falls 89.35% to 87.97%. Observed 20/20 is not a deployment-reliability lower bound.[12]

JOLT: train the teacher to meet the student's capabilities

A Good Self-Teacher Meets the Student Where They Are: Joint On-Policy Learning and Teaching ↗
Randy Ardywibowo · Arnav Dalal · Jiantao Jiao
New blog October 21; paper arXiv:2610.10447 v1 first posted October 7

One model jointly learns as a privileged teacher and unprivileged student. Teacher rewards are regularized toward the student; JOLT+ adds student rewards. At 63.44% MATH accuracy, GRPO's observed 8.40M processed tokens are compared with JOLT's fitted 1.30M. The 6.5-fold token comparison is not wall-clock acceleration.[13]

OPAL: rewrite Muon / AdamW recipes during a training run

Reactive Optimizers ↗
Xingjian Bai · Jia-Hao Liang · Jayden Teoh · Vincent Sitzmann
Page datePublished / dateModified both October 21

An agent rewrites groupwise update code from training statistics, including Muon switches, momentum changes, and loss-spike guards. Multilingual NanoGPT and diffusion experiments compare available one-run recipes; RAEv2-B FID improves 3.39 to 3.19. Agent tokens and decision overhead must be counted; uniform compute superiority is not established.[14]

Why does unregularized GD select maximum margin?

The Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent on Separable Data ↗
Daniel Soudry · Elad Hoffer · Mor Shpigel Nacson · Suriya Gunasekar · Nathan Srebro
First posted 2017-10-27; JMLR 2018; read the binary sections of arXiv v7 (2024-10-26)
JMLR 2018 · Foundational historical work on implicit bias ↗

Hard-margin SVM explicitly selects small norm; separable logistic regression has no finite minimizer. Soudry et al. (2018) connect them: GD's norm diverges while its direction is selected.[15][16]

Boundary and discussion

The general directional rate is slow; nondegenerate data improve it to O(1/log t). Arbitrary deep networks and Muon are not covered. When P_t changes, what testable conditions preserve a fixed-norm max-margin characterization?[15]
