How does stabilization change the update near zero?

Last-Iterate Stationarity of Muon with Finitely Many Newton-Schulz Iterations ↗
Ping Li · Vincent Y. F. Tan
First posted 2026-10-25 · v1

Let F be lower bounded with globally L-Lipschitz gradient in Frobenius norm. The paper studies finite-step NS in exact arithmetic, without weight decay, rather than replacing it by polar.[1]

Equal fit quality need not mean equal forecasts

Ambiguous Fits: The Rashomon Effect and Challenges in Scaling Law Estimation ↗
Bruno Mlodozeniec · Spencer Frei
First posted 2026-10-25 · v1

Chinchilla (2022) fitting commonly uses multi-start local optimization. This paper separates global optimization accuracy from agreement among nearly optimal extrapolations.[2]

x denotes resource axes, y observed loss; h_delta is Huber loss with threshold delta [2]
L(x;ϑ)=E+∑i=1kAixi−αi,R(ϑ)=∑nhδ ⁣(log⁡L(xn;ϑ)−log⁡yn)L(x;\vartheta)=E+\sum_{i=1}^k A_ix_i^{-\alpha_i},\qquad R(\vartheta)=\sum_n h_\delta\!\left(\log L(x_n;\vartheta)-\log y_n\right)L(x;ϑ)=E+i=1∑k​Ai​xi−αi​​,R(ϑ)=n∑​hδ​(logL(xn​;ϑ)−logyn​)
Notation for explanation: Theta is a declared compact domain; the envelope covers predictions of near-optimal fits [2]
Sϵ={ϑ∈Θ:R(ϑ)≤inf⁡ΘR+ϵ},[L−(x∗),L+(x∗)]=[inf⁡SϵL(x∗;ϑ),sup⁡SϵL(x∗;ϑ)]\mathcal S_\epsilon=\{\vartheta\in\Theta:R(\vartheta)\le\inf_{\Theta}R+\epsilon\},\qquad [L_-(x_*),L_+(x_*)]=\left[\inf_{\mathcal S_\epsilon}L(x_*;\vartheta),\sup_{\mathcal S_\epsilon}L(x_*;\vartheta)\right]Sϵ​={ϑ∈Θ:R(ϑ)≤Θinf​R+ϵ},[L−​(x∗​),L+​(x∗​)]=[Sϵ​inf​L(x∗​;ϑ),Sϵ​sup​L(x∗​;ϑ)]

A monotone reparameterization bounds residuals over parameter boxes. Branch-and-bound splits promising boxes and discards those whose lower bounds cannot beat the incumbent. Stopping certifies its gap to a global lower bound. Four real scaling ladders demonstrate divergent extrapolation with nearly equal fit error.[2]

Evidence boundary

The envelope describes optimization ambiguity for fixed data, model family and parameter domain. It is not a confidence interval for future observations and does not cover training randomness or model misspecification.[2]

Predictability also depends on the RL metric

An Empirical Study of Reinforcement Learning Scaling Laws ↗
Zeyu Zheng · Clare Lyle · Khimya Khetarpal · Will Dabney
First posted 2026-10-23 23:29 New York time · v1; delayed-index catch-up

In Starpilot and Craftax actor–critic experiments, authors find a single-agent Elo variant more predictable than mean return and report extrapolation across two orders of magnitude in compute. Craftax compute-optimal model size can be non-monotone. Fit/extrapolation splits were not checked.[4]

Druv Pai · Dejan Stancevic · Yi Ma · Luca Ambrogioni
First posted 2026-10-23 18:21 New York time · v1; delayed-index catch-up

Model information measures sample dependence on the particular training dataset. Authors posit two scaling regimes and derive induced laws for memorization and quality. This is a proposed, empirically fitted law; the abstract does not establish universality.[5]

Depth benefits depend on the task spectrum

Depth Improves Training-Time Scaling for Difficult Tasks in Deep Linear Networks ↗
Taishi Watanabe · Ryo Karakida · Jun-nosuke Teramae
2026-10-26 · v1

In deep linear networks, depth improves the training-time exponent for difficult tasks but not easy ones; predictor-norm time reparameterization yields a common law. Abstract only: initialization, spectral conditions and nonlinear numerical evidence were not checked in detail.[6]

Sparse structure is not enough: which initialization survives?

The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis: Finding Sparse, Trainable Neural Networks ↗
Jonathan Frankle · Michael Carbin
First posted 2018-03-09; read v5 (2019-03-04), ICLR 2019
ICLR 2019 Best Paper Award ↗

Pruning (LeCun et al., 1990; Han et al., 2015) showed that trained networks can be compressed. The lottery hypothesis asks whether the subnetwork trains independently from its original initialization. Let d count parameters and m be a fixed binary mask.[12][13]

Weights outside the mask remain zero [12]
θ0∼D,m∈{0,1}d,fm(x;θ)=f(x;m⊙θ)\theta_0\sim\mathcal D,\quad m\in\{0,1\}^d,\qquad f_m(x;\theta)=f(x;m\odot\theta)θ0​∼D,m∈{0,1}d,fm​(x;θ)=f(x;m⊙θ)
Original hypothesis: j is the minimum-validation-loss iteration and a its test accuracy [12]
∃m:∥m∥0≪d,jm≤jdense,am≥adense\exists m:\quad \|m\|_0\ll d,\qquad j_m\le j_{\rm dense},\qquad a_m\ge a_{\rm dense}∃m:∥m∥0​≪d,jm​≤jdense​,am​≥adense​
Algorithm restatement: train, magnitude-prune, reset each round; trained values are not retained [12]
θ^(r)=Train⁡(mr⊙θ0),mr+1=mr⊙KeepLarge⁡(θ^(r)),θstart(r+1)=mr+1⊙θ0\hat\theta^{(r)}=\operatorname{Train}(m_r\odot\theta_0),\qquad m_{r+1}=m_r\odot\operatorname{KeepLarge}(\hat\theta^{(r)}),\qquad \theta_{\rm start}^{(r+1)}=m_{r+1}\odot\theta_0θ^(r)=Train(mr​⊙θ0​),mr+1​=mr​⊙KeepLarge(θ^(r)),θstart(r+1)​=mr+1​⊙θ0​
Key control fixes the mask and resamples initialization, separating structure from initialization [12]
f(x;m⊙θ0)vs.f(x;m⊙θ0′),θ0′∼Df(x;m\odot\theta_0)\quad\text{vs.}\quad f(x;m\odot\theta'_0),\qquad \theta'_0\sim\mathcal Df(x;m⊙θ0​)vs.f(x;m⊙θ0′​),θ0′​∼D

MNIST fully connected and CIFAR10 convolutional experiments support the hypothesis. Original initialization generally beats reinitializing the same mask. Deep networks are learning-rate sensitive: VGG-19 at 0.1 did not yield winning tickets without warmup, but did with it. Iteration savings exclude repeated mask discovery.[12]

Evidence boundary

Evidence concerns the tested training settings. Discussion: replacing SGD by Muon, do initialization sensitivity and mask transferability change separately?
