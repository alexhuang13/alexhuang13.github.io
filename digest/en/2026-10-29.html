Predicting mixtures, identifying gains

Meta Models · Limits of data curation · Reading LoRA

Two main readings, two briefs, and three version watches recovered in the overlapping window: predicting experiments from training records and separating acceleration from persistent curation gains. The classic is LoRA and its factorized gradients.

Treat mixture experiments as numeric regression

Meta Models: Models that Model Learning ↗
Peihang Li · Ziming Liu
First submitted 2026-10-27 · v1 · Recovered in the overlapping window

Building on parametric scaling curves (Kaplan, 2020; Chinchilla, 2022), this paper supplies configurations q, progress t, and historical losses to frozen TabPFN. Mixture proportions are configuration fields.[1]

The prediction target; context updates do not train weights [1]
R={(qi,tij,Lij)}i,j,L^(q,t)=fθ(q,t∣R)\mathcal R=\{(q_i,t_{ij},L_{ij})\}_{i,j},\qquad \widehat L(q,t)=f_\theta(q,t\mid\mathcal R)R={(qi​,tij​,Lij​)}i,j​,L(q,t)=fθ​(q,t∣R)

Leaving out five-domain mixtures individually, MAE is about 20%–40% below the best evaluated parametric law at four model sizes; laws win on two-domain mixtures and some RedPajama settings.[1]

Boundary

These are new-mixture predictions at fixed scale and progress, without guarantees of training gains or arbitrary scale extrapolation. Group holdout is not prospective experimental validation.[1]

Reaching it faster, or reaching a better endpoint?

Advancement or Acceleration? How Data Curation Improves LLM Training ↗
Jiaxing Wang · Zicheng Zhang · Deping Xiang · Jin Xu · Yong Li · Chao Liu · Pengzhang Liu · Qixia Jiang
First submitted 2026-10-27 · v1 · Recovered in the overlapping window

The paper analyzes growing sample budgets in teacher–student perceptrons and distinguishes the effects empirically in LLMs. If the population training objective equals target loss and the baseline is globally optimal, reallocation cannot beat that optimum; finite-budget acceleration remains possible.[2]

Population optimality without shift; not a nonconvex training guarantee [2]

Domain-level diagnostic; training loss is measured on seen examples [2]
Δ=∑k∣w^kLtark(θ0)−wkLtraink(θ0)∣\Delta=\sum_k\left|\widehat w_k L^k_{\rm tar}(\theta_0)-w_k L^k_{\rm train}(\theta_0)\right|Δ=k∑​​wk​Ltark​(θ0​)−wk​Ltraink​(θ0​)​
Boundary

Δ is neither a distributional distance nor a statistical test with a significance level: different distributions can yield equal loss. Persistent finite-budget gaps do not prove infinite-training limits.[2]

Sources and diversity at small data scale

Specialization, Diversity, and Scaling in Child-Scale Language Models ↗
Steven Y. Feng · Michael C. Frank
First submitted 2026-10-27 · v1 · Recovered in the overlapping window

Based on the abstract: small GPT-2 models use BabyView home-recording transcripts and synthetic TinyDialogues, varying quantity, source, and diversity of child environments. Multiple children improve pooled held-out language prediction, without equally clear grammatical-benchmark gains; one loss does not represent every capability.[3]

Effective critical exponents for grokking

Scaling and Renormalization in Neural Networks: An Effective Renormalization-Group Description of Grokking ↗
Min Chen · Junqi Wang · Zeguan Wu · Wenhao Huang · Junyu Liu
First submitted 2026-10-26 · v1 · Recovered in the overlapping window

Based on the abstract: in two-layer modular-addition networks, a second-order loss expansion in per-neuron norms defines couplings, followed by Fiedler ordering and coarse-graining to estimate critical exponents. This concerns scaling of ML generalization transitions, rather than a universal model–data–compute budget power law.[4]

Low rank also changes the optimization path

LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models ↗
Edward J. Hu · Yelong Shen · Phillip Wallis · Zeyuan Allen-Zhu · Yuanzhi Li · Shean Wang · Lu Wang · Weizhu Chen
First submitted 2021-06-17; v2 (2021-10-16), §§4, 7 and appendix rank experiments
ICLR 2022 · Important historical work on parameter-efficient adaptation (DBLP checked 2026-10-29) ↗

Full fine-tuning stores all parameters per task; adapters (2019) add layers. Following low-intrinsic-dimension adaptation (2020), LoRA freezes pretrained weights and learns a low-rank increment that can be merged for deployment.[5][6]

Paper parameterization; W₀ is frozen [5]
W=W0+sBA,s=α/r,B∈Rd×r, A∈Rr×k,r≪min⁡(d,k)W=W_0+sBA,\quad s=\alpha/r,\quad B\in\mathbb R^{d\times r},\ A\in\mathbb R^{r\times k},\quad r\ll\min(d,k)W=W0​+sBA,s=α/r,B∈Rd×r, A∈Rr×k,r≪min(d,k)
Per-matrix parameter count and representation constraint [5]
#(A,B)=r(d+k),rank⁡(W−W0)≤r\#(A,B)=r(d+k),\qquad \operatorname{rank}(W-W_0)\le r#(A,B)=r(d+k),rank(W−W0​)≤r

Let G denote the loss gradient with respect to the effective weight. The following uses the chain rule and a first-order expansion of simultaneous SGD. The paper uses Adam, so this recurrence does not directly describe its experiments.

Explanatory derivation; not a paper theorem
∇BL=sGA⊤,∇AL=sB⊤G\nabla_B L=sGA^\top,\qquad\nabla_A L=sB^\top G∇B​L=sGA⊤,∇A​L=sB⊤G
Induced matrix update under simultaneous SGD; factors set left/right preconditioning
ΔW=−ηs2(GA⊤A+BB⊤G)+O(η2)\Delta W=-\eta s^2(GA^\top A+BB^\top G)+O(\eta^2)ΔW=−ηs2(GA⊤A+BB⊤G)+O(η2)
Paper initialization; first-step SGD consequence
B0=0,A0∼N:∇AL∣0=0,ΔW0=−ηs2G0A0⊤A0B_0=0,\quad A_0\sim\mathcal N:\qquad \nabla_A L|_0=0,\quad\Delta W_0=-\eta s^2G_0A_0^\top A_0B0​=0,A0​∼N:∇A​L∣0​=0,ΔW0​=−ηs2G0​A0⊤​A0​
Boundary

Low-rank sufficiency is empirical and task-specific, not a theorem that full-tuning gradients remain low rank. The best rank depends on the metric in GPT-2 E2E experiments; transfer of fixed rank across models needs testing.[5]
