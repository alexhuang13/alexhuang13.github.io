The return to looping depends on what is held fixed

How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference ↗
Chanakya Ekbote · Artidoro Pagnoni · Devendra Singh Sachan · Shruti Bhosale · Mike Lewis · Maryam Fazel-Zarandi · Shashank Rajput · Sujan Kumar Gonugondla
First posted 2026-10-29 · v1 · overlap-window addition

From Universal Transformer (2018) to PLT (2025), weight sharing adds effective depth, but every loop still executes. This study compares loop placement, specialization, and parallel interfaces in pretraining from scratch.[1]

Schematic shared transform and Loop LoRA; the latter relaxes exact sharing by loop [1]
hn(ℓ)=FW(hn(ℓ−1)),W(ℓ)=W+BℓAℓh_n^{(\ell)}=F_W(h_n^{(\ell-1)}),\qquad W^{(\ell)}=W+B_\ell A_\ellhn(ℓ)​=FW​(hn(ℓ−1)​),W(ℓ)=W+Bℓ​Aℓ​

Experiments span 70M–1B parameters, up to 155B tokens and 32 loops. Full-stack looping generally favors fixed parameter budgets; middle-stack looping is more competitive at fixed training FLOPs. Lower bits per byte (BPB) is better.[1]

Raw cross-token interface: previous-token states remove same-token serial dependence [1]
bn(ℓ)=en+hn−1(ℓ−1)b_n^{(\ell)}=e_n+h_{n-1}^{(\ell-1)}bn(ℓ)​=en​+hn−1(ℓ−1)​

The repair preserves the current embedding, separately RMS-normalizes both streams, and controls their mixture. The 200M ablation uses fixed 50/50 mixing; subsequent 100M–1B experiments use a rank-8 linear gate and separate KV caches.[1]

Boundary

Parameter efficiency need not imply compute efficiency; a parallel dependency graph alone does not establish end-to-end wall-clock speedup. Loop gains can saturate, reverse, or become unstable.[1]

Brief: predict value before paying execution cost

Does Scaling Toward AGI Depend on What System-1 Lets System-2 Execute? ↗
Qi Wang · Yun Qu · Lizhou Cai · Heming Zou · Haoang Chi · Yiqin Lv · Xiang Li · Haiyan Feng · Mengyu Ge · Yize Geng · Yuhang Jiang · Yixiu Mao · Xiangyang Ji
First posted 2026-10-29 07:00 UTC (October 29 in New York) · v1

This Perspective proposes a lightweight predictor that screens interactions before an expensive executor acts, then updates from outcomes. Ideal savings depend on useful-interaction density; prediction errors, exploration, and drift erode them.[2]

Illustrative accounting: K equal-cost candidates, admitted fraction ρ, prediction/execution costs c_p/c_e; not the full paper theorem

Boundary

This accounting assumes screening preserves the required utility; the ratio approaches 1/ρ only when prediction is negligible. The paper acknowledges that gating censors training observations, so i.i.d. error rates need not apply. Informative compute remains a testable scaling hypothesis.[2]

The interpolation threshold moves with training

Deep Double Descent: Where Bigger Models and More Data Hurt ↗
Preetum Nakkiran · Gal Kaplun · Yamini Bansal · Tristan Yang · Boaz Barak · Ilya Sutskever
First posted 2019-12-05; read v1 §§2 and 4–7; ICLR 2020
ICLR 2020 · important historical double-descent work (DBLP checked 2026-10-31) ↗

Belkin et al. (2018) connected classical and interpolating regimes through double descent. This paper extends the axis from parameter count to the training procedure: one architecture can cross the critical regime as training continues.[3][4]

Paper definition: largest sample size fitted by procedure T with expected training error ≤ε [3]

Paper hypothesis: increasing effective complexity helps away from criticality but may hurt near it; not a universal theorem [3]
EMC(T)≪n ;EMC(T)≈n ;EMC(T)≫n\mathrm{EMC}(\mathcal T)\ll n\ ;\quad \mathrm{EMC}(\mathcal T)\approx n\ ;\quad \mathrm{EMC}(\mathcal T)\gg nEMC(T)≪n ;EMC(T)≈n ;EMC(T)≫n

Neither ε nor “sufficiently far” from criticality is principled in EMC. The experiments and hypothesis do not guarantee double descent for every deep network; the linear illustration does not imply decreasing risk whenever p>n.[3]
