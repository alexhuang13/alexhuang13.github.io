How we’re training a Figma-fluent editing model ↗

Technical blog · 2026-10-29 · Overlap catch-up

Teachers generate starting-design/request/successful-edit triples. Deterministic validators and visual judges filter candidates; feedback repairs edits. An early Llama 3.3 70B used over 250,000 examples; later work adds other bases and RL.[2]

Explanatory notation: several evaluation dimensions, not a disclosed reward formula
q(y;x)=(vstructure,vvisual,vrequest)q(y;x)=(v_{\rm structure},v_{\rm visual},v_{\rm request})q(y;x)=(vstructure​,vvisual​,vrequest​)

Structural checks, visual assessment and designer calibration provide separate evidence. SFT teaches native file syntax, but the authors report generalization limits. RL algorithms and reward weights are undisclosed.[2]

Boundary

Speed, undo rates and ratings reflect changes across the whole agent, including tools and harness. They do not isolate RL or the editing model’s contribution.[2]

GPT-2/C4 experiments vary size, unique data and passes, with cosine schedules matched to run length. A few passes nearly match fresh data; longer repetition saturates. This does not imply that training becomes useless immediately after four passes.[4]

Boundary

Saturation is a fitted-model prediction, not an information-theoretic impossibility. Quality, schedules and architecture require revalidation; the law does not transfer directly to RL rollouts.[4]
