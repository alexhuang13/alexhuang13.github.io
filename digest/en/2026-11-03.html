Extrapolation must look beyond pretraining

When small models fail, make the task easier first

Hinted Scaling Laws ↗
Suze van Adrichem · Luke Bailey · Rohith Kuditipudi · Christine Ye · Neil Band · Ahmed Ahmed · John Duchi · Percy Liang · Tatsunori Hashimoto
First posted 2026-11-01 17:50 EST · v1

Near-zero small-model accuracy hides extrapolation signals. The authors introduce a difficulty axis H through truncated/masked reference solutions or teacher-logit mixing, then extrapolate to the unhinted task H=0.[1]

Empirical fit; C is log₁₀ FLOPs, or log₁₀ parameter count for post-trained families—not raw compute [1]
L(C,H)=max⁡{F, l+(A−l)sigmoid⁡(β1C+β2H+β3)}L(C,H)=\max\{F,\ l+(A-l)\operatorname{sigmoid}(\beta_1 C+\beta_2 H+\beta_3)\}L(C,H)=max{F, l+(A−l)sigmoid(β1​C+β2​H+β3​)}
Logit hint: teacher sees a reference solution and shares the tokenizer; F is the floor, l/A are sigmoid asymptotes, and β are fitted coefficients [1]
sH=zmodel+Hzteacher,yt∼softmax⁡(sH/T),L^=L(Clarge,0)s_H=z_{\rm model}+H z_{\rm teacher},\qquad y_t\sim\operatorname{softmax}(s_H/T),\qquad \widehat L=L(C_{\rm large},0)sH​=zmodel​+Hzteacher​,yt​∼softmax(sH​/T),L=L(Clarge​,0)

Lower pretraining loss need not yield a better fine-tuning frontier

Pretraining Data Scale Reverses the Muon–AdamW Fine-Tuning Gap ↗
Catherine Li · Jingchu Gai · Andrej Risteski · Jacob Mitchell Springer · Aditi Raghunathan
First posted 2026-11-02 13:56 EST · v1

OLMo-style models of 30M–600M parameters, pretrained on 150M–154B DCLM tokens, are compared on new-task loss versus pretraining forgetting. Increasing data can reverse Muon’s advantage under both checkpoint-selection protocols.[2]

D denotes pretraining tokens, N parameters; the frontier measures fine-tuning loss and forgetting jointly [2]
TPR⁡=D/N,ΔLPT=LPT(θFT)−LPT(θPT)\operatorname{TPR}=D/N,\qquad \Delta\mathcal L_{\rm PT}=\mathcal L_{\rm PT}(\theta_{\rm FT})-\mathcal L_{\rm PT}(\theta_{\rm PT})TPR=D/N,ΔLPT​=LPT​(θFT​)−LPT​(θPT​)
Paper margin–spread ratio: mean margins alone do not determine robustness; their dispersion also matters [2]

Crossover points depend on the task. Theory uses Gaussian keys/values, zero initialization and one full-batch GD versus exact-polar Muon step in a linear model—not a general AdamW or nonlinear-LLM theorem.[2]

Student-generated rollouts can still forget

Learning new skills without forgetting: Self-Distillation vs GRPO ↗
Chaitanya Ahuja · Sebastian Zuba
Technical blog dated 2026-11-02

Full-weight Nemotron Nano 9B training, with thinking off in the main experiment, proceeds through tool use → science → Knights & Knaves. Self-distillation matches token distributions to a demonstration-conditioned teacher on student rollouts; GRPO uses verifiable rewards.[3]

Mechanism schematic: d is a demonstration; ΔR measures each retained capability j; no KL direction is asserted
pS(⋅∣x,y<t)⟷pT(⋅∣x,d,y<t),ΔRj=Rj(θfinal)−Rj(θbase)p_S(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\longleftrightarrow p_T(\cdot\mid x,d,y_{<t}),\qquad \Delta R_j=R_j(\theta_{\rm final})-R_j(\theta_{\rm base})pS​(⋅∣x,y<t​)⟷pT​(⋅∣x,d,y<t​),ΔRj​=Rj​(θfinal​)−Rj​(θbase​)

Mixed training reduces self-distillation’s logic-theme drop from about 30 to 4 pp. Even lenient formatting leaves a 37-pp BBH logic decline, so formatting does not explain everything.[3]

Boundary

Iterations are matched, not wall-clock time or total compute; GRPO’s multiple rollouts carry additional cost. One model/task chain does not establish universal RL superiority.[3]

Rikiya Takehi · Jayden Teoh · Alex Zhang · Omar Khattab

GPT-6 Astra edits 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B weights to reduce looping, raising MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%, with 50 batched forward-feedback calls. Gradient-free does not mean evaluation-free. Separate denoising, loss weighting and the solver

Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models ↗
