Stronger regularization does not win everywhere

Prescriptive Scaling Laws for Data Constrained Training ↗
Justin Lovelace · Christian Belardi · Srivatsa Kundurthy · Shriya Sudhakar · Kilian Q. Weinberger
First posted 2026-05-02; v2 2026-11-04 18:24 EST · delayed catch-up

With fixed data, extra training can reduce fitting error while increasing repetition damage. The existing framework adds an overfitting term to the Chinchilla (2022) form. This version adds a full FineWeb-Edu validation and λ=0.5 and 3.0. The formula is existing methodology; the table and boundary describe new evidence. [1–3]

One-parameter penalty: U is unique tokens, R extra epochs beyond the first and N parameter count; five base-law parameters are fitted separately [1]
D=U(1+R),Lλ(N,U,R)=Eλ+AλN−αλ+BλD−βλ+PλR N/UD=U(1+R),\qquad L_\lambda(N,U,R)=E_\lambda+A_\lambda N^{-\alpha_\lambda}+B_\lambda D^{-\beta_\lambda}+P_\lambda R\,N/UD=U(1+R),Lλ​(N,U,R)=Eλ​+Aλ​N−αλ​+Bλ​D−βλ​+Pλ​RN/U

P falls with λ, while the single-epoch base loss rises. Greater repetition tolerance therefore does not imply superiority at every budget. The added FineWeb-Edu sweep gives one-parameter P=0.02182, near FineWeb’s 0.02305: a local check across two corpora. [1]

Illustrative allocation: 6ND is a parameter-token FLOPs approximation; the paper uses a more flexible four-parameter penalty for configuration selection [1]
min⁡N,R,λLλ(N,U,R)subject to6NU(1+R)≤C\min_{N,R,\lambda}L_\lambda(N,U,R)\quad\text{subject to}\quad 6NU(1+R)\le CN,R,λmin​Lλ​(N,U,R)subject to6NU(1+R)≤C
Boundary

The λ=0.1 grid differs slightly from the others, and base-law parameters are refitted for each λ. P ratios do not isolate a causal weight-decay effect. The added corpus sweep reaches 1B parameters and 16 epochs; extrapolation remains unverified. [1]

Why a fourth root on each side?

Shampoo: Preconditioned Stochastic Tensor Optimization ↗
Vineet Gupta · Tomer Koren · Yoram Singer
ICML 2018 Oral; first arXiv posting 2018-02-26; version read: official ICML 2018 paper
ICML 2018 Oral · official record checked 2026-11-08 ↗

Full-matrix AdaGrad (2011) stores second-order statistics of flattened mn-dimensional gradients, requiring m²n² storage. Shampoo retains matrix structure and stores m²+n² left/right statistics. Its theory concerns online convex losses, not nonconvex deep-network convergence. Let Gₜ=∇fₜ(Wₜ)∈R^(m×n), with ε>0. [4–6]

Algorithm 1: accumulated left/right Gram statistics, not Hessians [4]

Paper’s row-major flattening: vecᵣ(ΔW)=−ηHₜ⁻¹vecᵣ(G), rather than two independent inverse square roots [4]
