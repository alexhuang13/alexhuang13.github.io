Matrix ownership and parallel small teams

DMuon‑2 · Parallel Agent Teams · PPO

Two readings examine whole-matrix updates in sharded training and why multiple small teams can beat independent agents or one large team. Briefs cover cooperative training, verifier noise, inference budgets and architecture scaling. The classic develops PPO’s one-sided clipping.

One owner per matrix, rather than orthogonalizing each shard

DMuon-2: Scaling Matrix Optimizers in Data Parallelism with Topology-Aware Matrix Ownership and Communication ↗
Starrick Liu · Ruizhi Chengze · Lufang Chen · Michael Zhao · Ran Yu · Xiaofan Li · Dance Kuzi · Yue Zhang · Zijuan Lin · SongAo Han · YongHua Chen · Ruyi Gan · Hao Wang
First posted 11-04 14:43 EST; version read: v2, 11-06 04:14 EST; 2026

FSDP/ZeRO shards commute with elementwise AdamW updates, but not generally with matrix orthogonalization. DMuon‑2 gathers each complete matrix to one owner, balances estimated compute, then publishes updates. [1][2]

Exact-polar illustration; implementation uses finite Newton–Schulz and checks correspondence to whole-matrix updates [1]
polar⁡ ⁣(M1M2)≠(polar⁡(M1)polar⁡(M2))in general\operatorname{polar}\!\begin{pmatrix}M_1\\M_2\end{pmatrix}\ne\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{polar}(M_1)\\\operatorname{polar}(M_2)\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{in general}polar(M1​M2​​)=(polar(M1​)polar(M2​)​)in general

HSDP shares owners and optimizer state across replicas. Each finished matrix can be published across nodes while later work continues. This reorganizes communication and ownership rather than the optimization objective. [1]

Boundary

LLM throughput runs use random tokens; VLA uses prepared LIBERO batches and excludes loading. These measure training-step throughput, not time to matched validation loss. [1]

Collaboration lifts the mean; multiple teams retain selection gains

Test-Time Compute Scales Better with Parallel Agent Teams ↗
Junlin Wang · Haozhe Chen · Ang Li · Rahul Chalamala · Zhuang Liu
2026-11-06 12:53 EST · v1

With budget B agents, each running for a fixed duration, k-agent teams provide N=B/k independent samples. Members share findings; a task verifier selects the best submission across teams. [3]

Paper’s statistical model: independent samples, with Zᵢ standard normal; a₁=0 [3]
Xk∼N(μk,σk2),Emax⁡i≤NXk,i=μk+σkaN,aN=Emax⁡i≤NZiX_k\sim\mathcal N(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2),\qquad \mathbb E\max_{i\le N}X_{k,i}=\mu_k+\sigma_k a_N,\quad a_N=\mathbb E\max_{i\le N}Z_iXk​∼N(μk​,σk2​),Ei≤Nmax​Xk,i​=μk​+σk​aN​,aN​=Ei≤Nmax​Zi​
Large-sample extreme-value approximation, not an accuracy guarantee at N=1 [3]
V(B,k)≈μk+σk2log⁡(B/k)V(B,k)\approx\mu_k+\sigma_k\sqrt{2\log(B/k)}V(B,k)≈μk​+σk​2log(B/k)​

For Qwen3.8‑27B at B=100, teams of ten beat independent agents on 10/12 long-horizon tasks and beat one team of 100 on all four tested tasks. The Gaussian model predicts the gain’s sign correctly on only 9/12 tasks. [3]

Boundary

Equal agent-duration budgets do not fix generated tokens or exact FLOPs. GPU-time and API-token dollar accounting are separate; results depend on verifiable submissions and sample diversity. [3]

Verifier noise changes search temperature, but quantitative predictions fail

Verifier Noise Is a Temperature: Testing Tilt Predictions in Finite-Budget LLM-Guided Search ↗
Xiaomeng Li
2026-11-06 11:44 EST · v1

Under reversible proposals and logistic score-difference noise, accepting improvements yields an exponentially tilted stationary law; caching incumbent scores changes the setting. Preregistered finite-budget program searches broadly support directions but miss predicted magnitudes. [5]

TTRL: more votes only address finite-sample uncertainty

Haoyu Wang , Ru Wang , Yuanyuan Wang , Jiaxian Guo , Mingming Gong
2026-11-06 13:49 EST · v1

Separate population-majority bias from prompt-specific finite-rollout variation; the former is generally unidentifiable without external supervision. Vote-conditioned robust DPO and caching address unstable updates and forgetting, not guaranteed convergence to true labels. [6]

CascadeRec: extra compute may not help cold users

Kai Zhao , Guilin Zhang , Xiang Cheng , Xiquan Cui , Henry Heng , Xu Chu , Alet Blanken

MS-pDiT adds scale streams rather than only depth

Ruibo Chen , Kaishen Wang , Tianyi Xiong , Chenxi Liu , Yanshuo Chen , Heng Huang , Jian Du

PPO clips incentives in the improving direction
