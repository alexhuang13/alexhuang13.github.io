Hypergradient spectra and hidden preference transfer

Three close readings connect matrix hypergradients, scaling with problem difficulty, and how regularization changes behavioral transfer. Briefs cover author updates and inference budgets; the classic uses an explicit scale to distinguish feature learning from linearized dynamics.

Polar updates of hypergradients

BiMuon: A Geometry-Aware Stochastic Bilevel Optimization Approach ↗
Zhen Qin · Zhiyao Zhang · Yingbin Liang · Jia Liu
v1 · 2026-11-06 15:06 EST; announced Nov 10

Implicit differentiation inserts inverse lower-level curvature into the upper-level gradient. BiMuon orthogonalizes momentum of a matrix hypergradient upstairs while tracking the lower-level solution with stochastic gradients. Derivatives below are matrix operators evaluated at the lower-level solution. [1]

Implicit hypergradient; μ-strong convexity downstairs ensures invertibility
Φ(X)=f(X,Y∗(X)),Y∗(X)=arg⁡min⁡Yg(X,Y),∇Φ=∇Xf−∇XY2g(∇YY2g)−1∇Yf\Phi(X)=f(X,Y^*(X)),\quad Y^*(X)=\arg\min_Y g(X,Y),\qquad \nabla\Phi=\nabla_X f-\nabla^2_{XY}g(\nabla^2_{YY}g)^{-1}\nabla_Y fΦ(X)=f(X,Y∗(X)),Y∗(X)=argYmin​g(X,Y),∇Φ=∇X​f−∇XY2​g(∇YY2​g)−1∇Y​f
Algorithm schematic; Ĥ is a stochastic Neumann hypergradient estimate
Mt=θMt−1+(1−θ)H^t,Xt+1=Xt−βpolar⁡(Mt)M_t=\theta M_{t-1}+(1-\theta)\widehat H_t,\qquad X_{t+1}=X_t-\beta\operatorname{polar}(M_t)Mt​=θMt−1​+(1−θ)Ht​,Xt+1​=Xt​−βpolar(Mt​)
Theorem 1: exact polar on the left, finite NS on the right; R is uniform over the first T iterates
E∥∇Φ(XR)∥∗≤CpolT−1/4,E∥∇Φ(XR)∥∗≤Cpol1−εqT−1/4\mathbb E\|\nabla\Phi(X_R)\|_*\le C_{\rm pol}T^{-1/4},\qquad \mathbb E\|\nabla\Phi(X_R)\|_*\le\frac{C_{\rm pol}}{1-\varepsilon_q}T^{-1/4}E∥∇Φ(XR​)∥∗​≤Cpol​T−1/4,E∥∇Φ(XR​)∥∗​≤1−εq​Cpol​​T−1/4
Evidence boundary

Requires sample-level lower strong convexity, Lipschitz/smoothness assumptions, fresh independent unbiased derivatives and specified stepsizes. Finite NS additionally requires a uniform operator error εq<1 throughout the run; a conventional fixed five steps does not automatically ensure it. ε⁻⁴ counts outer iterations, not end-to-end speedup. [1]

Power-law loss does not remove exponential problem-size scaling

Semiprime Factorization with Discrete Diffusion and Beam Search: Scaling Laws ↗
Brendan O’Donoghue · Nenad Tomašev · Volodymyr Mnih · Timothy Nguyen · Dalin Guo · Arun Nair · Kenneth Yang · Alexandre Fréchette · Jasper Xian · Jean Tarbouriech · Pavel Tyletski · Ingrid von Glehn · Johannes Bausch · Demis Hassabis · Raia Hadsell
v1 · 2026-11-06; announced Nov 10

Given a semiprime n, discrete diffusion predicts two factors of at most k bits. Repeated remasking on one trajectory and beam search are distinct decoding protocols. Scaling is studied separately in data, model size, denoising steps and k. [2]

Empirical fits: s counts training steps; the last relation approximates a fixed low-loss trend, not a complexity lower bound
n=pq,p,q<2k;L(s)=As−α+L∞,s∗(k+1)≈2s∗(k)n=pq,\quad p,q<2^k;\qquad L(s)=A s^{-\alpha}+L_\infty,\qquad s_*(k+1)\approx2s_*(k)n=pq,p,q<2k;L(s)=As−α+L∞​,s∗​(k+1)≈2s∗​(k)

For k=15–18, another factor bit needs roughly twice as many training steps. More inference compute helps models already achieving nonzero success, but did not produce exact factors with standalone k=34 decoding. [2]

Evidence boundary

The 68-bit record is one success combining a diffusion candidate with classical lattice postprocessing, not high solve rates or superiority over classical factoring. Small-k data repeat examples and prime factors; held-out pairs do not imply generalization to unseen primes. [2]

Constraining adaptation can amplify out-of-domain preferences

Regularization Amplifies Subliminal Learning ↗
Lawrence Feng · Jacob Mitchell Springer · Gaurav R. Ghosal · Ziqian Zhong · Aditi Raghunathan
v1 · 2026-11-06 21:08 EST; announced Nov 10

A teacher receives a preference prompt but outputs only number sequences. The student never sees that prompt and only runs SFT; transfer is measured outside the training domain. Lower LoRA rank, parameter penalties and more unique data can each strengthen transfer. [3]

Schematic mechanisms of two constraints, not one objective covering every experiment

Evidence boundary

Learning rates are tuned by rank. The table measures direct preference answers, not training accuracy or general safety. Trend strength differs across targets and transfer protocols. Preference complexity determining optimal regularization remains an author hypothesis. [3]

How a large scale freezes features

On Lazy Training in Differentiable Programming ↗
Lénaïc Chizat · Edouard Oyallon · Francis Bach
First posted 2018-12-19; NeurIPS 2019; read v5: 2020-01-07
NeurIPS 2019 · foundational work on feature learning and kernel limits ↗

NTK (2018) connects wide networks to kernels. This paper shows that width is not the only route to lazy training: explicit output scaling can make a model behave like its initial tangent space. Let h map parameters to a Hilbert prediction space, with locally Lipschitz Dh and Lipschitz gradient of risk R. [13][14]

α⁻² preserves an appropriate dynamical timescale; nonlinear and tangent models share initialization
Fα(w)=α−2R(αh(w)),hˉ(w)=h(w0)+Dh(w0)(w−w0)F_\alpha(w)=\alpha^{-2}R(\alpha h(w)),\qquad \bar h(w)=h(w_0)+Dh(w_0)(w-w_0)Fα​(w)=α−2R(αh(w)),hˉ(w)=h(w0​)+Dh(w0​)(w−w0​)
Chain rule: the Jacobian kernel preconditions prediction-space gradient flow
w˙=−α−1Dh(w)∗∇R(αh(w)),u=αh(w),u˙=−K(w)∇R(u),K(w)=Dh(w)Dh(w)∗\dot w=-\alpha^{-1}Dh(w)^*\nabla R(\alpha h(w)),\quad u=\alpha h(w),\quad \dot u=-K(w)\nabla R(u),\quad K(w)=Dh(w)Dh(w)^*w˙=−α−1Dh(w)∗∇R(αh(w)),u=αh(w),u˙=−K(w)∇R(u),K(w)=Dh(w)Dh(w)∗
Equivalent solution for the tangent model: exponential decay in positive-eigenvalue directions; nullspace residual remains
Kˉ=Dh(w0)Dh(w0)∗,R(u)=12∥u−y∥2  ⟹  uˉ(t)−y=e−Kˉt(uˉ(0)−y)\bar K=Dh(w_0)Dh(w_0)^*,\qquad R(u)=\tfrac12\|u-y\|^2\;\Longrightarrow\;\bar u(t)-y=e^{-\bar Kt}(\bar u(0)-y)Kˉ=Dh(w0​)Dh(w0​)∗,R(u)=21​∥u−y∥2⟹uˉ(t)−y=e−Kˉt(uˉ(0)−y)
Theorem 2.2: h(w₀)=0, fixed finite T, α→∞; constants can depend on T
sup⁡t≤T∥wα(t)−w0∥=O(α−1),sup⁡t≤T∥wα(t)−wˉα(t)∥=O(α−2),sup⁡t≤T∥αh(wα(t))−αhˉ(wˉα(t))∥=O(α−1)\sup_{t\le T}\|w_\alpha(t)-w_0\|=O(\alpha^{-1}),\quad\sup_{t\le T}\|w_\alpha(t)-\bar w_\alpha(t)\|=O(\alpha^{-2}),\quad\sup_{t\le T}\|\alpha h(w_\alpha(t))-\alpha\bar h(\bar w_\alpha(t))\|=O(\alpha^{-1})t≤Tsup​∥wα​(t)−w0​∥=O(α−1),t≤Tsup​∥wα​(t)−wˉα​(t)∥=O(α−2),t≤Tsup​∥αh(wα​(t))−αhˉ(wˉα​(t))∥=O(α−1)

Proof route: control parameter displacement, bound kernel drift through local Lipschitzness of Dh, then compare the flows with Grönwall. Strongly convex risk and a surjective initial Jacobian enable a separate all-time result; it does not follow directly from fixed-T bounds. CNN experiments show degraded performance in the lazy regime, not a theorem that every feature-learning model is better. [13]
