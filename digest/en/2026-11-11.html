Coverage collapse, allocation drift, and robust Muon

Reference resets · Finite-budget scaling · Byzantine-Robust Muon · D3PM

Three distinctions anchor this issue: mean reward versus the probability of an optimal response, local slopes versus asymptotic power laws, and absolute performance under attack versus the extra degradation due to attack. Briefs include new work by Wharton authors; the classic dissects D3PM transition kernels.

Reward can rise while optimal responses vanish

A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets ↗
Audrey Huang · Nan Jiang · Sham M. Kakade · Sadhika Malladi · Akshay Krishnamurthy · Dylan J. Foster
v1 · 2026-11-10 13:31 EST

Treat a full response as an action in a contextual bandit, with prompt distribution μ and optimal-response set Y*. Coverage cost averages the draws needed to see a first optimal response on each prompt. Mean reward does not determine this quantity. [1]

Smaller coverage cost is better; the two quantities track optimal answers and average performance respectively

Reward still increases strictly during the collapse phase of the counterexample. Exact inner solves accumulate exponential tilts relative to the initial policy; approximate inner solves require additional error control. [1]

Evidence boundary

The lower bound also covers KL regularization to a fixed initial reference. Positive results concern softmax-linear policies over frozen features and the paper’s PPO/Kimi/Rebel variants under stated conditions; they do not directly explain every full-model LLM training curve. [1]

Aggregate momentum before choosing matrix update geometry

Byzantine-Robust Muon: Resilient Momentum Orthogonalization for Distributed Learning ↗
Grigory Malinovsky · Yury Demidovich · Peter Richtárik
v1 · 2026-11-09 14:18 EST

Honest workers send local momentum. The server robustly aggregates it into M, then minimizes a linear objective in a spectral-norm trust region. Aggregation errors are measured in the dual nuclear norm, separating adversarial estimation from update geometry. [3]

Algorithm 1: exact polar supplies a solution; malicious workers can send arbitrary values

Relative heterogeneity B and absolute offset ζ; δ bounds the Byzantine fraction, c is the aggregation constant, and δ̄ parameterizes oracle error
1G∑i∈G∥∇fi−∇f∥∗≤B∥∇f∥∗+ζ,1−δˉ−4cδB>0\frac1G\sum_{i\in\mathcal G}\|\nabla f_i-\nabla f\|_*\le B\|\nabla f\|_*+\zeta,\qquad 1-\bar\delta-4c\delta B>0G1​i∈G∑​∥∇fi​−∇f∥∗​≤B∥∇f∥∗​+ζ,1−δˉ−4cδB>0

The positive quantity is the denominator of the nonconvex average-stationarity bound. Under an approximate oracle, the adversarial numerator terms are unchanged, but the smaller denominator can raise the error floor. [3]

Evidence boundary

Table 6 reports five-seed means for a 38.15M byte model at 1,200 rounds, seven honest workers and two benign or Byzantine messages, all using unbucketed RFA. Muon has lower absolute perplexity; the difference in degradation relative to each benign baseline remains uncertain. Five NS steps do not certify the oracle condition. [3]

Put discrete noise into the transition kernel
