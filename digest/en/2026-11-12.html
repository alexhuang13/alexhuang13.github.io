Identifying scaling laws, balancing MoE, and training small agents

TPP coverage · Muon and first-layer routing · BotPsy · RWKV

A fixed data ratio can fit a curve without separating the effects of data and parameters. Two other close readings examine first-layer routing under Muon and a small agent’s post-training recipe. The classic derives RWKV’s normalized recurrence.

On FineWeb-Edu with 55M active parameters, first-layer bias span tracks the backbone optimizer more than the router optimizer. Centering only the router inputs strongly reduces early overload while leaving expert inputs intact. [3]

Evidence boundary

The main mechanism and centering control already appear in v1; the core measurements in v2 are not new findings. Results from two small models, a particular router and short tuning runs do not establish inevitable imbalance in large-scale MoE training. [3][4]

Learn tool use, then recover general capabilities

Qwen3.5-0.8B first receives tool-trajectory SFT, then on-policy distillation from three 4B specialists in tool use, reasoning and instruction following. Two DAPO rounds then emphasize tool calls and broaden to other verifiable tasks. [5]

Mechanism schematic: student-generated prefix sₜ and one teacher T(x) per prompt; unspecified loss weights are omitted

The first RL round gives partial credit for tool names, argument names and values. Figure 4 shows knowledge and instruction-following scores falling after SFT and recovering during distillation. [5]

Evidence boundary

The table compares the whole pipeline with its base, not compute-matched stage ablations. It cannot isolate the contributions of teacher size, data mixture and RL. [5]

AFT (2021) aggregates values with positional biases and key weights without pairwise query–key dot products. RWKV restricts those biases to channelwise exponential decay, compressing history into numerator and denominator states. The notation below is for one channel. [14][15]

Keys and values are kᵢ and vᵢ; ρ controls the channel’s memory
