Noise turns the sign discontinuity into a local slope

When and How SignSGD Outperforms SGD: A Functional Scaling Law Perspective ↗
Zilin Wang · Binghui Li · Jia-Nan Wang · Lean Wang · Jinbo Wang · Lei Wu
v1 · 2026-11-12 04:47 EST

Let g be one coordinate of the true gradient, a its single-sample noise standard deviation, and B the batch size. Under the Gaussian noise surrogate, mean sign responds nearly linearly when the gradient is small relative to batch noise. [1]

Scalar expansion from §6.1; the response saturates for large signals
B−1/2E[sign⁡(g+aZ/B)]=B−1/2[2Φ(gB/a)−1]≈2/π g/a,Z∼N(0,1)B^{-1/2}\mathbb E[\operatorname{sign}(g+aZ/\sqrt B)]=B^{-1/2}\bigl[2\Phi(g\sqrt B/a)-1\bigr]\approx\sqrt{2/\pi}\,g/a,\quad Z\sim N(0,1)B−1/2E[sign(g+aZ/B​)]=B−1/2[2Φ(gB​/a)−1]≈2/π​g/a,Z∼N(0,1)
With diagonal features and curvature-aligned noise variance, the local drift has square-root curvature
ai2≍λi(H),gi=λi(H)ei⟹gi/ai≍λi(H)eia_i^2\asymp\lambda_i(H),\quad g_i=\lambda_i(H)e_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_i/a_i\asymp\sqrt{\lambda_i(H)}e_iai2​≍λi​(H),gi​=λi​(H)ei​⟹gi​/ai​≍λi​(H)​ei​
Theorem 3.1: intrinsic time t; s and q come from the source and spectral-decay conditions; n_B is noise injection
E[Et]≍S(t)+∫0tK(t−τ)nB(τ) dτ,S(t)≍t−2s,K(t)≍t−(2−2/q)\mathbb E[\mathcal E_t]\asymp S(t)+\int_0^tK(t-\tau)n_B(\tau)\,d\tau,\qquad S(t)\asymp t^{-2s},\quad K(t)\asymp t^{-(2-2/q)}E[Et​]≍S(t)+∫0t​K(t−τ)nB​(τ)dτ,S(t)≍t−2s,K(t)≍t−(2−2/q)
Evidence boundary

Signal error decays faster and noise is forgotten more slowly than for SGD, yielding different comparisons in hard and easy regimes. The theorem concerns a diagonal power-law regression SDE under curvature alignment and stepsize and time conditions. Pretraining results do not establish the same mechanism for matrix Muon. [1]

Start more prefixes and finish fewer paths

Early Signals, Strong Decisions: Prefix-Guided Sampling for Parallel Test-Time Scaling ↗
Jie Ren · Jonathan Rosenfeld · Neil Thompson
v1 · 2026-11-10 22:18 EST

PreG samples reasoning prefixes of length k, ranks them by internal confidence, and completes only the top fraction α. Let T be full path length and B the generation-token budget; the expression ignores rounding and variable path lengths. [2]

A smaller α filters more aggressively but reduces the number of completed paths
B=Mprek+Mcomp(T−k),Mcomp=αMpre=αBk+α(T−k)B=M_{\rm pre}k+M_{\rm comp}(T-k),\quad M_{\rm comp}=\alpha M_{\rm pre}=\frac{\alpha B}{k+\alpha(T-k)}B=Mpre​k+Mcomp​(T−k),Mcomp​=αMpre​=k+α(T−k)αB​
Proposition 1 approximation for independent bounded path margins with mean μ and variance σ²; explains aggregation without asserting independence after ranking
Pr⁡(correct wins)≈Φ(M μ/σ)\Pr(\text{correct wins})\approx \Phi(\sqrt M\,\mu/\sigma)Pr(correct wins)≈Φ(M​μ/σ)
Evidence boundary

Screening is most useful near the decision boundary, but a single rule does not improve every benchmark. This is inference-time answer selection: dropping low-confidence prefixes changes the sampling distribution and provides no unbiased policy-gradient estimator. [2]

The local rate comes from linearization

The normalization denominator has a positive offset b₀,₁. As momentum vanishes, finitely many odd polynomial steps act locally like scalar multiplication. This is different from exact polar flattening of all nonzero singular values. [3]

First-order term from Lemma 2.7; the positive coefficient is absorbed into the effective stepsize

Optimal local χ-rate of Theorem 1.2, with χ the Hessian condition number at the minimum
ρMuon=ρmomentum=χ−1χ+1,ρGD=χ−1χ+1\rho_{\rm Muon}=\rho_{\rm momentum}=\frac{\sqrt\chi-1}{\sqrt\chi+1},\qquad \rho_{\rm GD}=\frac{\chi-1}{\chi+1}ρMuon​=ρmomentum​=χ​+1χ​−1​,ρGD​=χ+1χ−1​
Evidence boundary

The theorem uses deterministic local analysis, positive definite Hessian and the specified normalization. It does not give the same rate for zero-offset normalization, exact polar or stochastic training far from the minimum, nor establish local acceleration over momentum. [3]

Distributed optimization as sign voting

signSGD: Compressed Optimisation for Non-Convex Problems ↗
Jeremy Bernstein · Yu-Xiang Wang · Kamyar Azizzadenesheli · Anima Anandkumar
First arXiv posting February 12, 2018 ET; read the ICML 2018 version
ICML 2018 · foundational one-bit gradient compression ↗

Taking coordinatewise signs lets each worker send one bit per coordinate. Gradient magnitudes are lost, so the analysis controls wrong-sign probabilities rather than reusing an unbiased-SGD proof. [19][20]

Equivalent identity with binary signs and no ties; a wrong sign reverses descent
qi=Pr⁡(sign⁡(g^i)≠sign⁡(gi)),E[gisign⁡(g^i)]=∣gi∣(1−2qi)q_i=\Pr(\operatorname{sign}(\hat g_i)\ne\operatorname{sign}(g_i)),\quad \mathbb E[g_i\operatorname{sign}(\hat g_i)]=|g_i|(1-2q_i)qi​=Pr(sign(g^​i​)=sign(gi​)),E[gi​sign(g^​i​)]=∣gi​∣(1−2qi​)
One-step bound: nominal descent, wrong-sign penalty, and curvature cost
E[f(x+)−f(x)]≤−η∥g∥1+2η∑i∣gi∣qi+η22∥L∥1\mathbb E[f(x^+)-f(x)]\le-\eta\|g\|_1+2\eta\sum_i|g_i|q_i+\tfrac{\eta^2}{2}\|L\|_1E[f(x+)−f(x)]≤−η∥g∥1​+2ηi∑​∣gi​∣qi​+2η2​∥L∥1​
Simplification for iid workers and odd m; voting suppresses error when qᵢ<1/2
qivote=Pr⁡(Binomial⁡(m,qi)>m/2)q_i^{\rm vote}=\Pr\big(\operatorname{Binomial}(m,q_i)>m/2\big)qivote​=Pr(Binomial(m,qi​)>m/2)

Under a lower bound, coordinatewise smoothness and variance conditions, Theorem 1 takes batch K and stepsize proportional to K⁻¹ᐟ², giving an expected average gradient ℓ₁ norm of O(K⁻¹ᐟ²). This uses K² stochastic gradient calls. The improved voting bound also uses symmetric unimodal noise. [19]
