When smaller batches weaken Muon’s descent

BlinkMimic: Training a Humanoid Motion Tracker in Minutes on a Single GPU ↗
Fuyi Chen
v1 submitted 2026-11-12 02:31 EST · announced Nov 13

BlinkMimic trains motion tracking for a simulated Unitree G1 through a differentiable world model. The appendix uses a local quadratic model to explain the batch-size knee, treating momentum as signal plus noise with entrywise variance inversely proportional to batch size B. [1]

Appendix A.4 heuristic; H is the local Hessian and the inner product is Frobenius
PB=polar⁡(Gˉ+EB),ℓ(W)−ℓ(W−ηPB)≈η⟨Gˉ,PB⟩−12η2⟨PB,HPB⟩P_B=\operatorname{polar}(\bar G+E_B),\qquad \ell(W)-\ell(W-\eta P_B)\approx\eta\langle\bar G,P_B\rangle-\tfrac12\eta^2\langle P_B,HP_B\ranglePB​=polar(Gˉ+EB​),ℓ(W)−ℓ(W−ηPB​)≈η⟨Gˉ,PB​⟩−21​η2⟨PB​,HPB​⟩

The appendix treats the curvature term as approximately constant. As B falls, progress per step shrinks rapidly when alignment approaches the curvature cost. After fitting at B=1,024, the preregistered prediction at B=512 was 2,280–2,630 iterations; one seed took 2,301. [1]

Evidence boundary

The full recipe reaches three consecutive clean checkpoints in 3.4 minutes on average on an RTX 4090 (40 LAFAN1 clips, four replicas each, mean return at least 0.82; timing excludes offline evaluation). This is a whole-recipe result, and the Adam swap holds the recipe’s step size and batch fixed. [1]

Separating the optimal loss surface from tuning penalties

ArchSpace: Recording, Reviewing, and Referencing LLM Architecture Research ↗
Jie Wang · Tong Zhu · JiaHao Kuang · Zhe Xu · Yunkai Chai · Dehong Zheng · Yu Cheng · Tao Ji · ZhouHan Lin · Qi Zhang · Bowen Zhou
v1 submitted 2026-11-12 13:59 EST · announced Nov 13

ArchSpace builds compute ladders for Qwen3.5 and OLMo3. Let M denote training FLOPs per token and D the token count. The fitted surface is minimized at fixed C=MD. For each model–budget pair, learning rate and batch are swept, and their best measured loss fits the base surface. [2]

Eq. (3); coupling exponent 1 recovers the additive form. The loss penalty for off-optimum hyperparameters is fitted separately

Empirical parameterization; the first term lets the best learning rate shift with batch size
ΔL=2h[cosh⁡(u−γη,Bv)−1+ρ(cosh⁡v−1)],u=log⁡(η/η∗),v=log⁡(B/B∗)\Delta L=2h\big[\cosh(u-\gamma_{\eta,B}v)-1+\rho(\cosh v-1)\big],\quad u=\log(\eta/\eta^*),\quad v=\log(B/B^*)ΔL=2h[cosh(u−γη,B​v)−1+ρ(coshv−1)],u=log(η/η∗),v=log(B/B∗)
Evidence boundary

With coefficients frozen, extrapolations along the fitted compute-optimal path up to ten times the largest fit budget yield Table 4 loss MAPE of 0.32%; the base surface alone gives 0.33%. These runs stay within the original tokens-per-parameter range. The authors ran a separate hyperparameter search for long training at about 130–160 tokens per parameter. [2]

Hera adds a Muon baseline for nonlinear forecasting

Training Dynamics of Softmax Self-Attention: Fast Global Convergence via Preconditioning ↗
Gautam Goel · Mahdi Soltanolkotabi · Peter Bartlett
v1 submitted 2026-03-02; v2 submitted 2026-11-11 06:59 EST · announced Nov 13

V2 adds one-step Lorenz–63 forecasting. Three softmax-attention layers read five successive states and a fixed readout predicts the next state. Hera updates two matrices independently in whitened coordinates, while Hera-PSD ties them together. [3][4]

Experimental parameterization; Muon and AdamW update the raw attention matrices
Hera-PSD:(U~,V~)=(B(I+K⊤K)−1,B)\text{Hera-PSD:}\quad (\widetilde U,\widetilde V)=\bigl(B(I+K^\top K)^{-1},B\bigr)Hera-PSD:(U,V)=(B(I+K⊤K)−1,B)

The train, validation, and test splits are consecutive blocks of 500 examples from one trajectory. Each method screens 44 configurations, with validation MSE used to select the long-run configuration. The table gives mean test MSE over five fresh initializations after 4,096 full-batch updates. [3]

Evidence boundary

The new results come from one dynamical-system trajectory. The finite-sample guarantee uses planted linear models and independent samples, and the table does not control for time per step. [3]

Synthetic data target class-conditional coverage

Dataset Distillation via Drifting ↗
George Cazenavette · Angelina Quan · Giannis Daras · Antonio Torralba · Vincent Sitzmann
v1 submitted 2026-11-12 06:17 EST · announced Nov 13

A frozen feature extractor defines attraction to real data and repulsion from other synthetic examples, optimizing images and their augmentations directly. On ImageNet-1k with 10 images per class, noise initialization and hard-label ResNet-18 training, the best MoCo-v3 variant reaches 27.0% test accuracy versus 11.7% for the strongest noise-initialized baseline. Coverage is feature-space dependent, and the paper reports same-encoder evaluation separately. [5]

Predicting a shared trajectory endpoint in one pass

Consistency Models ↗

First arXiv posting 2023-03-02 ET; read the ICML 2023 version
ICML 2023 · 2,480 Semantic Scholar citations (2026-11-15) ↗

Score SDE (2021) sends a high-noise sample back to the data end along a deterministic probability-flow ODE, typically requiring multiple network evaluations. Consistency Models learn each trajectory’s endpoint directly, with the identity map at the low-noise boundary. Let t be the Gaussian noise standard deviation and pₜ the perturbed marginal. [8][10]
