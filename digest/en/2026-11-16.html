Temporary weight decay and momentum under shuffling

Muon pretraining · without-replacement finite sums · inference compute · randomized labels

Two new papers examine how weight norms carry the effect of early decay and why momentum in without-replacement training depends on the problem. Briefs cover multi-agent budget comparisons and the form of pretraining data. The classic revisits randomized labels.

Why does weight decay still matter thousands of steps after removal?

Delayed Effects of Temporary Weight Decay in Muon Pretraining ↗
Md Rifat Arefin · Ravid Shwartz-Ziv · Ernie Chang · Chinnadhurai Sankar · Rylan Conway · Aristide Baratin · Irina Rish · Adithya Sagar · Patrick Huber

The intervention applies decoupled weight decay to hidden weight matrices during a short window. Early decay reduces weight norms, while Muon normalizes matrix update norms. Once decay ends, the runs follow the same update rule, but smaller weights still take larger steps relative to their size. [1]

A decay window covering the first 44% of steps, selected at 162M, was transferred directly to 1.4B (FineWeb100B, 28,000 updates). Its validation loss was 0.26 nats above no decay when the window ended. It crossed the baseline at 73% of training and finished 0.0275 nats lower. A separate comparison fixes the window at the first 10% of training. [1]

Evidence boundary

Norm resets at 162M and 405M support weight norm as the mediator of most of the gain. The recurrence describes persistent step-size differences. Final loss is measured in the training comparisons. [1]

With fixed problem constants and D₀>0, setting 1−β proportional to T to the power −1/3 and η proportional to T to the power −2/3 balances all three terms at order T to the power −1/3. The counterexample takes N=4K+1, with one component gradient locally equal to N and the rest equal to −1. A run of K negative inputs flips momentum when β < (N+1)⁻¹⁄ᴷ, causing net epoch motion in the positive full-gradient direction. [2]

Evidence boundary

Complexity counts individual component updates. The counterexample holds for every permutation when the step size within an epoch is constant and at most 1/(2N). It limits a universal choice of fixed momentum. [2]

A finite-size optimum for security anomaly detection

Condensing Law of Security Information ↗
Qi Tan · Tianyu Cui · Qi Li · Ke Xu
v1 submitted 2026-11-13 at 03:57 EST

The authors propose that a larger model may more readily reconstruct an anomalous input as a common pattern, erasing anomaly information in its intermediate representation. The abstract reports information-theoretic bounds linking restoration error, representation retention, and task error, and proposes a U-shaped error curve as a function of model size. [3]

Separate agent coordination gains from independent sampling gains

More Agents, More Samples, or More Thinking? Untangling Coordination, Ensembling, and Test-Time Compute in Multi-Agent LLMs ↗
Seyed Mahmoud Sajjadi Mohammadabadi
v1 submitted 2026-11-12 at 19:18 EST · Nov 16 listing

This synthesis of nine studies distinguishes single-pass, sample-matched, declared-resource-matched, and Pareto-frontier comparators. Across the sample- or resource-matched contrasts, multi-agent accuracy averaged 4.2 percentage points below the baseline. Equal sample counts can leave token use, latency, and cost unequal. The two positive Pareto contrasts are reported separately. [4]

Verbatim repetition and varied expressions teach different category knowledge

LECAT: Measuring LLM Category Learning from Pretraining Data ↗
Jimin Mun · Maarten Sap · Yanai Elazar
v1 submitted 2026-11-13 at 11:59 EST · Nov 16 listing

The authors distinguish memorizing category properties, generalizing to new entities, and applying knowledge in context. They analyze open models on 199 category–feature pairs and train a 68M model from scratch with controlled data expressions. The abstract reports that exact repetition reduces flexible use, while mixing different forms of expression improves it. [5]

Random labels can be interpolated. Why do true labels generalize?

Understanding Deep Learning Requires Rethinking Generalization ↗

arXiv v1 2016-11-10 ET · ICLR 2017 conference version
ICLR 2017 Oral ↗

The paper compares the same network trained with true labels and fixed random labels. On CIFAR-10, Inception without data augmentation or weight decay reached 100% training accuracy in both cases. Its test accuracies were 85.75% and 9.78%, respectively. This comparison separates fitting the sample from generalizing beyond it. [6]
