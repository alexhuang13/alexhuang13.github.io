Latent Feedback, Distillation Optimizers, and Inference Audits

Repeated training · Muon distillation · Adaptive inference · Recurrent networks

At fixed parameter count and training FLOPs, when does latent feedback beat more passes over the data? What happens to Muon in short-run distillation? A third study bounds risk after searching inference budgets. The classic revisits gradient explosion and clipping in RNNs.

Spend extra compute on latent feedback or more epochs?

Latent Feedback Is a Compute and Data Amplifier ↗
Benhao Huang · Chaitanya Dwivedi · Xuezhe Ma · Alexander Moreno · Hector Liu
v1 submitted November 15, 2026 ET · November 17 announcement

Feed the previous token’s top-layer state back into the current input. Training approximates this recurrence with one or two parallel passes and computes loss and gradients only on the last pass. The comparison holds fixed non-embedding parameters N, unique tokens U and FLOP budget F, measured in vanilla Transformer epochs over U. [1]

Feedback uses cₛ times the baseline’s training FLOPs per token, leaving Rₛ epochs under the same budget.
ut=et+Wrms⁡(ht−1),Rs=F/csu_t=e_t+W\operatorname{rms}(h_{t-1}),\qquad R_s=F/c_sut​=et​+Wrms(ht−1​),Rs​=F/cs​
Paper’s fitted law. For Rₛ<1, Dₛ=URₛ. The capacity multiplier vₛ is 1 for the vanilla Transformer.
Ls=E+[(A(vsN)α)q+(BDsβ)q]1/q,Ds=U[1+R∗(1−e−(Rs−1)/R∗)](Rs≥1)L_s=E+\left[\left(\frac{A}{(v_sN)^\alpha}\right)^q+\left(\frac{B}{D_s^\beta}\right)^q\right]^{1/q},\qquad D_s=U\left[1+R^*\left(1-e^{-(R_s-1)/R^*}\right)\right]\quad(R_s\ge1)Ls​=E+[((vs​N)αA​)q+(Dsβ​B​)q]1/q,Ds​=U[1+R∗(1−e−(Rs​−1)/R∗)](Rs​≥1)

To first order, the capacity multiplier lowers the capacity term, while extra forward passes reduce repeat exposure and raise the data term. The Dolma 3 comparison below matches model size and validation loss, using a budget of two vanilla Transformer epochs and varying unique tokens per non-embedding parameter. [1]

Evidence boundary

Fitting on 60M–271M models yielded at most 0.29% relative validation-loss error at the held-out 778M size. The comparison constrains deployed model size. If parameter count can grow, a larger equal-compute Transformer has 0.006–0.012 lower loss throughout the fitted grid. [1]

All-2D Muon shows no consistent advantage in short-run KL distillation

Do Pretraining Optimizer Gains Transfer to KL Distillation? ↗
Lavinia Ghita · Dhruv Desai
v1 submitted November 14, 2026 ET · November 17 announcement

Four instruction-tuned Qwen pairs share a tokenizer within each pair. The token-level forward KL compares the fixed teacher q with student p. Training averages the answer region A and reasoning region C separately within each example, so longer reasoning does not take more weight from the answer. [2]

Training objective with both regions present. Validation pools all supervised tokens with equal weight.
ℓit=DKL(qit∥pθ,it),Li=12mean⁡t∈Aiℓit+12mean⁡t∈Ciℓit\ell_{it}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{it}\|p_{\theta,it}),\qquad L_i=\tfrac12\operatorname{mean}_{t\in A_i}\ell_{it}+\tfrac12\operatorname{mean}_{t\in C_i}\ell_{it}ℓit​=DKL​(qit​∥pθ,it​),Li​=21​meant∈Ai​​ℓit​+21​meant∈Ci​​ℓit​

The protocol uses 79,535 Nemotron reasoning traces, global batch 144, 1,683 steps and three seeds. All-2D applies spectral updates to embeddings and the output head as well. The table shows comparisons explicitly reported in the paper. [2]

Evidence boundary

All completed matrix recipes took more trainer time, which includes teacher inference, validation and checkpointing. The result is for the fixed budget, All-2D parameter assignment and offline traces. Hidden-layer-only Muon and downstream accuracy were outside the comparison. [2]

How to bound risk after selecting the sampling budget on calibration data?

Anytime-Valid PAC-Bayes Certificates for Adaptive Test-Time Scaling ↗
Xiaoyu Li · Jiangxuan Long
v1 submitted November 16, 2026 ET · November 17 announcement

A recipe is a randomized predictor combining prompts, sampling budgets, allocation rules and verifiers. The prior P is independent of calibration data. Q may be chosen using the i.i.d. calibration problems and execution records. For losses between 0 and 1, summing fixed-time failures δ/[n(n+1)] gives one event covering every n and Q. [3]

Theorem 4.1. Selecting one of M recipes under a uniform prior costs log M / n. Invert binary kl for the risk upper bound.
kl⁡(L^n(Q)∥L(Q))≤DKL(Q∥P)+log⁡(2n n(n+1)/δ)n\operatorname{kl}(\widehat L_n(Q)\|L(Q))\le \frac{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|P)+\log(2\sqrt n\,n(n+1)/\delta)}{n}kl(Ln​(Q)∥L(Q))≤nDKL​(Q∥P)+log(2n​n(n+1)/δ)​
Simpler bound from Proposition 6.1 for b independent samples and answer support at most m. If a wrong answer is more probable, the probability of voting correctly instead has an exponentially decaying upper bound.
Pr⁡{voteb(x)≠y∣x}≤(m−1)e−bΔx2/2,Δx=px(y)−max⁡a≠ypx(a)>0\Pr\{\mathrm{vote}_b(x)\ne y\mid x\}\le(m-1)e^{-b\Delta_x^2/2},\qquad \Delta_x=p_x(y)-\max_{a\ne y}p_x(a)>0Pr{voteb​(x)=y∣x}≤(m−1)e−bΔx2​/2,Δx​=px​(y)−a=ymax​px​(a)>0
Evidence boundary

On GSM8K, gpt-4.1-mini selected b=5 from four budgets. With 659 calibration problems, the upper bound was 0.109 and the risk on 660 held-out problems was 0.039. The uniform bound can be loose. The prior and family of recipes must be fixed before inspecting calibration records. [3]

Randomized trial of instructor-designed AI

Instructor-designed AI improves learning in a randomized trial across 19 universities ↗
Jun Li · Robert Bray · Sébastien Martin · Hamsa Bastani (Wharton) · et al.
v1 submitted November 13, 2026 ET · November 17 announcement

Across 19 universities and about 7,200 students, course-level randomization and a difference-in-differences regression using historical outcomes gave a student-level exam effect of +0.32 SD (95% CI [0.06, 0.59]). Activity and satisfaction showed no significant change. The treatment let instructors design and deploy tools and encompassed heterogeneous interventions. [4]

Learning rules change the size of circuits implementing the Fourier algorithm

Grokking Can Be Steered: Selecting Desirable Solutions By Choice Of Learning Rule ↗
Pascal Sager · Benjamin Grewe · Pau Vilimelis Aceituno · Thilo Stadelmann
v1 submitted November 14, 2026 ET · November 17 announcement

On modular addition, the rules that grok recover a Fourier algorithm. Neuron-level credit equalization or rescaled decay increases effective circuit size from 60% to 99%. Muon, Adam and Shampoo yield more concentrated circuits under the tested setup. [6]

Why RNN gradients explode and what clipping controls

On the difficulty of training recurrent neural networks ↗
Razvan Pascanu · Tomas Mikolov · Yoshua Bengio
First arXiv posting November 21, 2012 · Read the ICML 2013 proceedings version
Highly cited in OpenAlex · Checked November 17, 2026 ↗

Bengio et al. (1994) tied the difficulty of learning long dependencies to gradients that decay across steps. This paper follows Jacobian products in BPTT and distinguishes backward contraction from oversized parameter updates near steep walls in the loss. [23]

The paper’s vanilla RNN parameterization, with input uₜ and hidden state xₜ.
xt=Wσ(xt−1)+But+b,Jt=∂xt/∂xt−1=Wdiag⁡(σ′(xt−1))x_t=W\sigma(x_{t-1})+Bu_t+b,\qquad J_t=\partial x_t/\partial x_{t-1}=W\operatorname{diag}(\sigma^{\prime}(x_{t-1}))xt​=Wσ(xt−1​)+But​+b,Jt​=∂xt​/∂xt−1​=Wdiag(σ′(xt−1​))
Row-gradient convention. If |σ′|≤Γ and the multiplier is below 1, the long-range error signal decays exponentially.
∂Et∂xk=∂Et∂xtJt⋯Jk+1,∥Jt⋯Jk+1∥2≤(∥W∥2Γ)t−k\frac{\partial E_t}{\partial x_k}=\frac{\partial E_t}{\partial x_t}J_t\cdots J_{k+1},\qquad \|J_t\cdots J_{k+1}\|_2\le (\|W\|_2\Gamma)^{t-k}∂xk​∂Et​​=∂xt​∂Et​​Jt​⋯Jk+1​,∥Jt​⋯Jk+1​∥2​≤(∥W∥2​Γ)t−k
Algorithm 1. The gradient direction is preserved and the Euclidean update is capped at ητ. A zero gradient remains zero.
g~=gmin⁡{1,τ/∥g∥2},θ+=θ−ηg~\widetilde g=g\min\{1,\tau/\|g\|_2\},\qquad \theta^+=\theta-\eta\widetilde gg​=gmin{1,τ/∥g∥2​},θ+=θ−ηg​
Soft constraint from Equation (9), encouraging norm preservation along the current nonzero backward direction. The implementation takes the immediate partial derivative, holding states and error signals fixed.

Evidence boundary

Clipping gives an update-norm cap. The norm-preserving penalty targets current error directions, and a long Jacobian product can still contract. [23]
