Curvature Diagnostics for Muon and Randomization Tests under Weak Nulls

Matrix optimization · Exact inference · Inference compute · Asynchronous post-training

The main reads examine curvature calibration of Muon’s spectral steps and finite-sample validity of Fisher tests for binary outcomes. Briefs cover inference by rewriting, expert replay, recurrence depth and post-training systems. The classic revisits GRPO in DeepSeekMath.

When can equal steps across singular directions be reallocated?

Equal Steps, Unequal Curvature: Where Muon's Update Goes Wrong ↗
Biswa Sengupta · Jinhua Wang · Léo Maxime Brunswic
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

Partition the proposed step D=ΣDᵦ by the singular-value ranks of training momentum, with parameter update −D. Independent held-out data estimate the gradient and curvature to diagnose the step allocation. [1]

Second-order local model of held-out loss. Muon uses c=1. If H is positive definite, the quadratic optimum is H⁻¹g.

At 124M parameters, FineWeb-Edu, 100.7M tokens and three paired seeds, splitting Q/K/V lowers final validation loss by 0.0250 nats versus fused QKV. A spectral tilt proportional to σ to the power −1/4 is then applied during cooldown. Increments below are against the stronger split-QKV baseline. [1]

Evidence boundary

At 355M, the combination gains 0.0188 nats. Budgets are below one token per parameter, and diagnostics overlapped early screening. The combination saves about 4.3% tokens but takes about 12% more time in the unfused implementation, leaving end-to-end acceleration unestablished. [1]

When does a Fisher test remain valid with heterogeneous individual effects?

On the Validity of Fisher Randomization Tests under the Weak Null ↗
Timothy Sudijono · Edgar Dobriban · Eric Tchetgen Tchetgen
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

Neyman (1923) targets a sample-average effect. Fisher’s (1935) standard randomization argument assumes zero effect for every unit. This paper fixes binary potential outcomes, uses only assignment randomness, and obtains finite-sample guarantees for three common designs. [2]

Under SUTVA, observe Yᵢ=Yᵢ(Aᵢ). The weak null allows positive and negative effects to cancel.
Yi(a)∈{0,1},τ=1n∑i[Yi(1)−Yi(0)],H0N:τ=0Y_i(a)\in\{0,1\},\qquad\tau=\frac1n\sum_i[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)],\qquad H_{0N}:\tau=0Yi​(a)∈{0,1},τ=n1​i∑​[Yi​(1)−Yi​(0)],H0N​:τ=0
For the three combinations, the two-sided result holds for all α∈(0,1). The one-sided guarantee for τ≤0 is stated for α≤1/2.
pF±=Pr⁡A′{∣T(A′,Yobs)∣≥∣T(Aobs,Yobs)∣},sup⁡H0NPr⁡(pF±≤α)≤αp_F^{\pm}=\Pr_{A^\prime}\{|T(A^\prime,Y^{\rm obs})|\ge|T(A^{\rm obs},Y^{\rm obs})|\},\qquad\sup_{H_{0N}}\Pr(p_F^{\pm}\le\alpha)\le\alphapF±​=A′Pr​{∣T(A′,Yobs)∣≥∣T(Aobs,Yobs)∣},H0N​sup​Pr(pF±​≤α)≤α

In complete randomization, conditioning on the observed event total O gives reference H∼Hypergeom(n,O,n/2). Counting compatible potential-outcome tables shows that the actual treated-event count is more concentrated, yielding tail control. [2]

Evidence boundary

The result depends on binary outcomes and the specified statistics. For a general exchangeable design, the paper gives a valid correction by doubling the one-sided p-value. Nonbinary outcomes admit counterexamples. [2]

Spend forward passes on equivalent rewritings

Test-Time Symmetrization: Scaling Laws for Model-Agnostic Learning under Structure ↗
Behrooz Tahmasebi · Melanie Weber
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

For finite-group rewritings such as graph relabeling, the authors approximate an orbit average. The abstract gives worst-case growth with log|G| and compares rewritings with repeated sampling at matched forward-pass counts. The law depends on the required approximation accuracy and does not imply exact recovery with a fixed number of calls. [3]

Reuse the chronological order of expert training logs

Replay, Don't Resample: Off-Policy Consolidation of Long-Horizon RL Experts ↗
Zhenrui Yue · Gongxiaohui Chen · Huimin Zeng · Honglei Zhuang · Jiashuo Sun · Zhen Han · Dong Wang
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

MRPD replays trajectories containing behavior probabilities and rewards in each expert’s training order, recomputes policy gradients and distills final experts at logged prefixes. The abstract reports better consolidation than online distillation on three long-horizon domains, without new environment calls. Log order matters more than amount or rewards. [4]

Component interactions need replicated comparisons

Do Frontier Model Ingredients Add Up? A Replicated Factorial Study of Small Language Models ↗
Biswa Sengupta
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

In a two-seed fractional factorial at shared model setting, data and budget, MoE, Muon and a Gated DeltaNet hybrid give larger average improvements. Some second- and third-order effects are aliased, and MoE has more total parameters. The design does not identify every higher-order interaction. [6]

A Differential Theory of Quality-Resource Trade-offs for AI Model Selection: The Multiresource Frontier and Its Minimax Price ↗
Dmitry Ignatov
v1 submitted November 15, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

The author develops a differential representation of error frontiers over latency, parameters, training compute and energy, deriving minimax prices under a channel model. A learning-curve surrogate accepts variable hyperparameter sets

ICICLE: In-Context Inference for Curve-Learning Extrapolation ↗
Laurits Fredsgaard · Michael Riis Andersen · Mikkel N. Schmidt
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

A 1.28M-parameter surrogate trained on synthetic priors supports freeze–thaw allocation using a shared set encoder for variable hyperparameter counts. Mean-regret trajectories resemble FT-PFN on LCBench, PD1 and TaskSet. Some Meta-Album subsets improve over Quick-Tune, while others show no clear difference. [8]

New tokens skip early loops while retaining deep context

Late Entry in Looped Language Models ↗
Ziqi Jin · Jiaxi Li · Weiwen Xu · Aixin Sun
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 19 announcement

Late entry runs new tokens through only the final K loops while completing old-token caches to depth T. Wake verifies candidates using completed states. Exact and lossy modes are reported separately. Ouro’s 5.2× exact-mode speedup is against the tokenwise baseline, versus +26% over full K=T block decoding. [9]

DaisyRose’s long-horizon tasks and asynchronous pipeline

DaisyRose-27B: Pushing the Frontier of Post-Training Efficiency ↗
Jason Chen · RadixArk
Published November 18, 2026

About 1,200 selected tasks were trained over 18 days on 144 H200s, with 128 for rollouts and 16 for the trainer. Sessions up to 1M tokens are split into trajectories through harness summaries, while native context stays at 256K. Terminal-Bench 4.1 pass@1 rises from 5.6 to 12.0. Reported benchmark scores select the higher BF16/FP8 result per benchmark. [10]

Evidence boundary

The team reports backend MFU of 37% versus 16% and end-to-end throughput of 16,800 versus 8,300 tokens/s. Multipliers depend on the compared backends and task settings, with independent-system replication still outstanding. [10]

SFT duration needs evaluation and checkpoint overheads

Why your fine-tuning job takes longer than the FLOPs say ↗
Bruno Duarte · Gabriel Pimenta de Freitas Cardoso · Isis F. Carvalho · Breno de Almeida Beleza · Felipe Lima
Published November 18, 2026

Across 272 H200 jobs covering LoRA and full SFT, the target is elapsed time from GPU allocation to completion, excluding queueing. CatBoost uses pre-dispatch configuration, cumulative evaluation volume and checkpoint volume. Stratified five-fold MAE is 1.71 hours versus 12.41 for the best tested FLOP baseline. Mean job duration is 23.43 hours. [11]

Evidence boundary

The data come from one production environment. Feature importance describes predictive associations, and transfer across hardware remains unverified. [11]

Dynamic sampling and prompt revisiting on FinQA

A 9B model beats a 1T model on FinQA, trained with Crusoe Managed RL ↗
Matan Halfon · Itay Niv · Daniel Shen · Suman Debnath · Yiwei Song · Omri Berkovitch
Published November 18, 2026

GRPO uses 16 rollouts per prompt. Half the prompts are fresh and half revisit unmastered prompts weighted by p(1−p), while equal-reward groups are filtered. Qwen3.5-9B rises from 53.1% to 73.9% on 290 external SnorkelFinance questions. Trained scores use each run’s peak checkpoint. [12]

Evidence boundary

The 35B-A3B ablations are single runs. The reported held-out run-to-run variation is about ±7 percentage points, leaving most ablation differences within that noise range. [12]
