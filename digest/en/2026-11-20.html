How Training Budgets Reshape Inference Scaling, and Non-Gaussian Sources Flow to Data

Budget internalization · Flow matching · Federated Muon · Deep linear dynamics

The main reads examine how fixed rollout caps reshape test-time scaling and how Euler discretization behaves in flow matching with non-Gaussian sources. Briefs cover spectral optimization, revised architecture and data-scaling studies, and post-training systems. The classic uses deep linear networks to resolve learning plateaus.

A training cap becomes a stopping habit

Budget Internalization: Fixed-Budget RLVR Trades Test-Time Scaling for In-Budget Accuracy ↗
Ilia Zavidnyi · Alexander Kovrigin · Arina Puchkova · Kirill Gelvan · Sergey Titov
v1 submitted November 19, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

A fixed rollout cap B also changes behavior: across Qwen3.5-4B, Gemma4-E2B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B, GRPO improves accuracy near the cap while contracting the benefits of longer reasoning. [1]

Simplified notation for censored rewards; A_B(b) is accuracy at evaluation cap b after training with cap B, not the full GRPO objective.
rB(y)=1{y is correct}1{y finishes within B},AB(b)=E[1{correct at evaluation cap b}]r_B(y)=\mathbf1\{y\text{ is correct}\}\mathbf1\{y\text{ finishes within }B\},\qquad A_B(b)=\mathbb E[\mathbf1\{\text{correct at evaluation cap }b\}]rB​(y)=1{y is correct}1{y finishes within B},AB​(b)=E[1{correct at evaluation cap b}]
The sign reversal below is an empirical finding, not a universal theorem.
ΔB(b)=AB(b)−Abase(b),Δ8k(8k)>0,Δ8k(64k)<0\Delta_B(b)=A_B(b)-A_{\rm base}(b),\qquad \Delta_{8k}(8k)>0,\quad \Delta_{8k}(64k)<0ΔB​(b)=AB​(b)−Abase​(b),Δ8k​(8k)>0,Δ8k​(64k)<0

The table uses Table 3 ablations: Qwen3.5-4B, DeepScaleR, an 8K training cap, at most 300 steps and 32 prompts × 8 rollouts per step. AIME 2024/25 contains 60 problems; avg@16 is mean correctness over 16 generations per problem (%), not pass@16. [1]

SFT on artificially truncated traces also reproduces internalization; naturally completed short traces do not show the same contraction. Strong KL and forced answers retain more high-budget capability while sacrificing in-budget gains. [1]

Evidence boundary

The ± values are problem-level 95% intervals. These ablations each use one training seed; the main DeepScaleR comparison separately uses three. Evidence implicates length censoring but does not establish an explicit internal representation of B. [1]

Non-Gaussian sources: which sampling error is controlled?

Flow Matching from Non-Gaussian Sources: A Theory of Efficient Sampling ↗
Yuchen Zhou · Lili Zheng · Yuxin Chen · Gen Li
v1 submitted November 19, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

Flow matching and rectified flow (2023) learn transport velocities by regression. Heavy-tailed or mixture sources are allowed; the question is how finitely many velocity evaluations control Euler error. [2]

Muon in heterogeneous federated CFD

Federated Learning of Car Aerodynamics Surrogates with Spectral Optimizers ↗
Arman Bolatov · Nikolay Spitsyn · Daniil Medyakov · Vladimir Aletov · Dmitry Bylinkin · Martin Takáč · Aleksandr Beznosikov
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

On six body-type clients, Transolver with FedMuon/FedMARS-M reaches drag error within 2% of centralized Muon using 2–4 local epochs per round. Momentum is corrected before orthogonalization. This application does not establish a general heterogeneous-convergence theory. [3]

GUI grounding: coverage versus selection

ScaleDiag: Diagnosing the Limits of Test-Time Scaling in GUI Grounding ↗
Chen Liu · Zhen Fang · Xin Yu · Dadong Wang · Ling Chen
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

Controlled changes to cropping, instruction information and candidate sampling separate bottlenecks. Correct coordinates often occur among samples, leaving 5.1–11.0 percentage points of oracle headroom; geometric consensus adds almost nothing. Abstract only; oracle headroom is not an achieved deployment gain. [4]

Evaluation-step curves for robot policies

CaRR reuses the existing edit trajectory

Closing Performance Gap for Diffusion Language Models under Low Inference Budgets ↗
Hao Wu · Sipeng Chen · Jingwen Fu · Yuwang Wang
v1 submitted November 19, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

CaRR refills replaced tokens and recovers submittable answers from the existing trajectory. At one-eighth of the full forward budget, mean accuracy over six metrics rises 7.44 percentage points. Abstract only; no extra backbone forwards does not imply zero system overhead. [6]

Scaling whole-body human-motion data

Awaken-HI: Scaling Humanoid Intelligence with Whole-Body Human Experience ↗
EngineAI
v1 submitted November 19, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

Human pretraining increases from 5,000 to 50,000 hours with robot adaptation data fixed, measuring action error and task success. Mean success is 93.75% across eight individual tasks, with only ten trials per task; this does not determine a cross-platform scaling exponent. [7]

One allocation rule for expert training and routing

Game-Theoretic Data Allocation for Expert Specialization and Routing ↗
Mark Lovett · Denis Lim · Soroush Vosoughi
v1 submitted November 18, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

InflEns solves a location game in fixed prompt-trait space to allocate LoRA training weights and routing shares. The highest-share expert has lowest held-out NLL on about 90% of prompts, versus about 15% after permutation. Evidence is limited to the studied safety corpus. [8]

CNNs: initial concentration versus kernel constancy

Deviations from the NTK Regime in CNNs under Joint Depth-Width Scaling ↗
Maria Matveev · Pascal Esser · Gitta Kutyniok · Mariia Seleznova
v1 submitted November 19, 2026 ET · November 20 announcement

At critical initialization, NTK dispersion approximately collapses against depth-to-width ratio L/C. Training-time kernel rotation also increases with this ratio, even under ordered initialization. Abstract only; numerical evidence does not establish a unified limit theorem. [9]

Wei Wei
Site date: November 19, 2026

Gemma 4 E2B trains for 100 steps on one TPU v6e-8 host, with 8 boards × 8 games per step. Reported held-out-board wins rise from 9.4% to 95%. The implementation uses GSPO-token loss, RLOO advantages and exact token IDs across tool turns. One small game does not establish general agent transfer. [16]

Deep linear networks: where do plateaus come from?

Evidence boundary

Exact solutions require whitening, alignment and continuous time. Numerical agreement for random initialization and nonlinear networks is not a theorem beyond these assumptions. Finite-step stability also constrains the interpretation of the depth limit. [18]
