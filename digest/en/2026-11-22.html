Denoising in Latent Space: What to Compress, What to Preserve

Separating compression error from generative error

High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models ↗
Robin Rombach · Andreas Blattmann · Dominik Lorenz · Patrick Esser · Björn Ommer
CVPR 2022 · First posted December 20, 2021; read v2: April 13, 2022
37,545 citations in the queried OpenAlex record (November 22, 2026) ↗

DDPM (2020) turns generation into iterative denoising, but each pixel-space step remains expensive. VQGAN (2021) compresses before learning a generative prior; LDM (CVPR 2022) retains a two-dimensional latent space and assigns generation to a convolutional U-Net, reducing the need for aggressive compression to make autoregressive modeling feasible. This is historical reading, excluded from today’s news count. [1] [2] [3]

Paper setting and dimension accounting: f is spatial downsampling and c the latent channel count. Spatial positions fall to 1/f²; total dimensionality or total cost need not fall by exactly that factor.

The first stage combines perceptual reconstruction with a patch discriminator to encourage local realism. It uses weak KL regularization or absorbs a VQ quantizer into the decoder. The second stage fixes E and D: KL latents are sampled and rescaled by an estimated standard deviation; VQ latents are noised before quantization. The empirical latent distribution need not itself be standard normal. [1]

Explanatory restatement, not a new theorem of the paper: the population MSE optimum is a conditional mean; differentiating the Gaussian-noised density gives the score identity. This requires t>0 and a differentiable conditional density; a finite network need not attain it.

Section 4.1 compares f on one A100 with the same parameter count and step count. Class-conditional ImageNet curves reach 2M steps; FID uses 100-step DDIM sampling. The analysis compares training progress and sampling throughput; equal steps do not imply equal elapsed time. [1]

My simplified cost accounting: generate n images with T denoiser calls and one decode each; autoencoder training can be amortized across tasks. Smaller spatial area is not an end-to-end speedup theorem.
