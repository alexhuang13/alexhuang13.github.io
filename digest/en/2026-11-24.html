Principal Directions, Random Coverage and Data Mixtures

DRAM · ProMix · Human-feedback summarization

Main reads examine DRAM’s subspace coverage and ProMix’s reference-model correction. Briefs cover rotational scheduling, inference compute and substantive extensions of earlier papers. The classic revisits human-feedback summarization, separating reward gains from human-preference gains.

DRAM: low rank should not retain only the strongest directions

DRAM: Memory-Efficient Muon via Rank Reallocation in Principal-Random Subspaces ↗
Bai, Haomin · Yu, Dingzhi · Zhao, Xuling · Yang, Sifan · Wang, Yibo · Zhang, Lijun
v1: November 21, 2026 ET; November 24 announcement

For X∈R^(m×n), take m≤n. Each DRAM block keeps the leading r left singular vectors R of a stochastic gradient and draws q uniform complementary directions P, with total rank k=r+q. Increasing the random share later addresses true-gradient directions missed when noise dominates TopSVD. [1]

The paper’s coverage lemma retains the principal subspace and covers its complement in expectation with strength δ; q>0 supplies this guarantee.
B=[R P],Π=RR⊤,δ=qm−r,E[BB⊤∣R]=Π+δ(I−Π)B=[R\ P],\quad\Pi=RR^\top,\quad\delta=\frac{q}{m-r},\qquad\mathbb E[BB^\top\mid R]=\Pi+\delta(I-\Pi)B=[R P],Π=RR⊤,δ=m−rq​,E[BB⊤∣R]=Π+δ(I−Π)
The paper’s within-block update; reset H=βĜ at block starts. Here β weights the gradient, unlike the common momentum-coefficient convention.
G^t=B⊤Gt,Ht=(1−β)Ht−1+βG^t,Xt+1=Xt−ηBpolar⁡(Ht)\widehat G_t=B^\top G_t,\quad H_t=(1-\beta)H_{t-1}+\beta\widehat G_t,\qquad X_{t+1}=X_t-\eta B\operatorname{polar}(H_t)Gt​=B⊤Gt​,Ht​=(1−β)Ht−1​+βGt​,Xt+1​=Xt​−ηBpolar(Ht​)

Persistent state falls from O(mn) to O((m+n)k), with spectral normalization on k×n momentum. C4 training uses 1.2B/2.5B/7B tokens for 60M/130M/350M models. The table reports mean accuracy over seven zero-shot tasks, not training loss. [1]

Evidence boundary

Rates concern the average nuclear norm of gradients, exact polar maps, unbiased finite-variance gradients and operator/nuclear smoothness, with nondecreasing coverage factors. They do not establish an end-to-end guarantee for finite NS. Best low-memory performance does not mean matching full-state Muon. [1]

ProMix: separate irreducible difficulty from learning headroom

ProMix: Efficient Data Mixture Optimization for Robotic Imitation via Proxy-Reference DRO ↗
Sima, Qie · Xue, Wei · Guo, Yike
v1: November 22, 2026 ET; November 24 announcement

Following the proxy/reference approach of DoReMi (2023) and robotic Re-Mix (2024), ProMix learns domain weights with small models. Lᵢ is mean behavioral-cloning loss on domain i; a frozen reference θR provides a baseline while proxy θP and domain distribution p are alternately optimized. [2]

The paper’s objective: high absolute loss may reflect noise; positive entropy in the maximization discourages weight collapse. The reference is not known Bayes risk.
min⁡θPmax⁡p∈ΔN∑ipi[Li(θP)−Li(θR)]+λH(p),H(p)=−∑ipilog⁡pi\min_{\theta_P}\max_{p\in\Delta_N}\sum_i p_i\bigl[L_i(\theta_P)-L_i(\theta_R)\bigr]+\lambda H(p),\qquad H(p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_iθP​min​p∈ΔN​max​i∑​pi​[Li​(θP​)−Li​(θR​)]+λH(p),H(p)=−i∑​pi​logpi​
The paper’s exponentiated-gradient update. Proxy search samples 128 examples from every domain each step, then weights by p; this is not an equal-cost step sampling a single domain from p.
gi=Li(θP)−Li(θR),pi+∝piexp⁡ ⁣{η[gi−λ(1+log⁡pi)]}g_i=L_i(\theta_P)-L_i(\theta_R),\qquad p_i^+\propto p_i\exp\!\left\{\eta\left[g_i-\lambda(1+\log p_i)\right]\right\}gi​=Li​(θP​)−Li​(θR​),pi+​∝pi​exp{η[gi​−λ(1+logpi​)]}

Reference and proxy each train for 100k steps. The 15M count covers trainable parameters, excluding frozen encoders. Weight search totals 11 GPU-hours; weights transfer to 50M and 7B VLAs without retuning. The paper reports 9,500 real-robot evaluations on Open-X: average success rises from 68.4% to 76.2%, and worst-domain success from 51.4% to 68.5%. [2]

Evidence boundary

The 11-versus-192 GPU-hour comparison concerns mixture search at 7B, not a 17× speedup of all VLA training. Transfer of small-model domain rankings is empirical in the tested tasks, not a general guarantee. [2]

Rotational schedules depend on parameter scale

From Learning Rates to Rotational Schedules: Finite-Horizon Theory on the Sphere ↗
Graldi, Jacopo · Orvieto, Antonio · Koloskova, Anastasia
v1: 2026-11-22 ET; November 24 announcement

For scale-invariant GD, the rotational coefficient is η/‖x‖². The text optimizes a last-iterate bound on a geodesically convex spherical cap, with an asymptotically linear-decay profile. Norm dynamics change the rotation induced by the same LR in ordinary Adam/Muon. This is not global nonconvex LLM convergence theory. [3]

Verifier audits: pass rates are not reliable completion

How to build and maintain an agentic evaluation ↗
Avijit Ghosh · Ben Burtenshaw · Irene Solaiman
First published November 23, 2026 (site date)

On eight synthetic-spectrum development tasks, checking only a peak-height ratio admits a shortcut that never fits the spectrum; fit checks are needed. Three-rollout pass@3 is 7/8, while success in all three runs, pass^3, is 1/8. These answer different reliability questions. The small development set does not establish deployment generalization. [27]

Why reward-model scores are not human preferences

Learning to summarize from human feedback ↗
Nisan Stiennon · Long Ouyang · Jeff Wu · Daniel M. Ziegler · Ryan Lowe · Chelsea Voss · Alec Radford · Dario Amodei · Paul Christiano
NeurIPS 2020; first posted September 2, 2020; read v3: February 15, 2022
3,702 citations in the queried OpenAlex record (November 24, 2026) ↗

After preference learning by Christiano et al. (2017) and language-model fine-tuning by Ziegler et al. (2019), this NeurIPS 2020 work joins comparison labeling, reward learning and PPO for summarization. Let x be a post and y a summary of at most 48 tokens. The target is labeler preference rather than ROUGE itself. [28]

The paper’s preference model: pairwise comparisons identify score differences, not calibrated absolute summary quality.
Pθ(yw≻yl∣x)=σ(rθ(x,yw)−rθ(x,yl)),LRM=−Elog⁡Pθ(yw≻yl∣x)P_\theta(y_w\succ y_l\mid x)=\sigma(r_\theta(x,y_w)-r_\theta(x,y_l)),\quad\mathcal L_{\rm RM}=-\mathbb E\log P_\theta(y_w\succ y_l\mid x)Pθ​(yw​≻yl​∣x)=σ(rθ​(x,yw​)−rθ​(x,yl​)),LRM​=−ElogPθ​(yw​≻yl​∣x)
The paper’s sampled reward and its expectation; KL references SFT, not the changing old policy. PPO updates the policy.
R(x,y)=rθ(x,y)−βlog⁡π(y∣x)πSFT(y∣x),J(π)=Ex ⁣[Eπrθ−βDKL(π∥πSFT)]R(x,y)=r_\theta(x,y)-\beta\log\frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm SFT}(y\mid x)},\quad J(\pi)=\mathbb E_x\!\left[\mathbb E_\pi r_\theta-\beta D_{\rm KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\rm SFT})\right]R(x,y)=rθ​(x,y)−βlogπSFT​(y∣x)π(y∣x)​,J(π)=Ex​[Eπ​rθ​−βDKL​(π∥πSFT​)]
Explanatory derivation for each x: with fixed reward, β>0, finite Z and compatible support, completing the KL yields the ideal optimum. This is not a paper theorem that finite PPO training attains it.
π∗(y∣x)=πSFT(y∣x)erθ(x,y)/βZ(x),Jx(π)=βlog⁡Z(x)−βDKL(π∥π∗)\pi^*(y\mid x)=\frac{\pi_{\rm SFT}(y\mid x)e^{r_\theta(x,y)/\beta}}{Z(x)},\qquad J_x(\pi)=\beta\log Z(x)-\beta D_{\rm KL}(\pi\|\pi^*)π∗(y∣x)=Z(x)πSFT​(y∣x)erθ​(x,y)/β​,Jx​(π)=βlogZ(x)−βDKL​(π∥π∗)
My error decomposition: u denotes ideal preference utility and e modeling error. Reducing β amplifies both; KL regularization does not automatically eliminate reward hacking.
rθ=u+e⟹π∗∝πSFTexp⁡(u/β)exp⁡(e/β)r_\theta=u+e\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\pi^*\propto\pi_{\rm SFT}\exp(u/\beta)\exp(e/\beta)rθ​=u+e⟹π∗∝πSFT​exp(u/β)exp(e/β)

The paper releases 64,832 TL;DR comparisons. PPO’s value network is separate from the policy and initialized from the reward model; all three have the same size in the described experiments. Section 4.3 varies optimization strength against an earlier reward model and finds eventual deterioration in human preference despite rising predicted preference. [28]

Evidence boundary

Read the method and overoptimization experiments; not reproduced. Preferences depend on instructions, labelers and summary distributions. Reward-validation accuracy and true utility after optimization are different metrics. [28]
