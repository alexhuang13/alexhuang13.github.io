Input injection: anchoring, bias and truncated gradients

How to build and train your recurrent-depth model: A fixed-point perspective ↗
Pethick, Thomas
v1: November 23, 2026 ET; November 25 announcement

Reusing shared layers increases inference depth, while training often backpropagates through only the final H steps. The paper identifies persistent injection of fixed input u with Halpern iteration: anchoring helps contraction but also changes the limit. [1]

The paper’s constant-step condition: fixed β can contract even for mildly expansive T. The limit is a fixed point of the anchored operator, generally not of T.
zk+1=βku+(1−βk)Tθ(zk),q=(1−β)Lip⁡(Tθ)<1z^{k+1}=\beta_k u+(1-\beta_k)T_\theta(z^k),\qquad q=(1-\beta)\operatorname{Lip}(T_\theta)<1zk+1=βk​u+(1−βk​)Tθ​(zk),q=(1−β)Lip(Tθ​)<1
The paper’s truncation bound requires trajectory differentiability, θ-independent u and z⁰, and uniformly B-bounded per-step parameter Jacobians. It is not unbiasedness of arbitrary training gradients.
∥∂θzK−(∂θzK)H∥≤BqH1−q\left\|\partial_\theta z^K-(\partial_\theta z^K)_{H}\right\|\le\frac{Bq^H}{1-q}​∂θ​zK−(∂θ​zK)H​​≤1−qBqH​

With nonexpansive T and a nonempty fixed-point set, among other conditions, βₖ=1/log(k+3) removes anchor bias. Absolute gradient error ε needs a window O(log K[log log K+log(1/ε)]). Lipschitz control of the neural parameterization motivates joint row- and spectral-norm constraints. [1]

Sparrow’s cheap feasible direction for jointly constrained parameter groups. Row normalizes each row to radius r; RowClip clips only oversized rows. Spectral is a spectral-ball linear oracle, not spectral clipping. The composition is not the exact joint LMO.
Kr,s={Z:max⁡i∥Zi,:∥2≤r, ∥Z∥op≤s},D=RowClip⁡r ⁣(Spectral⁡s(Row⁡r(M)))\mathcal K_{r,s}=\{Z:\max_i\|Z_{i,:}\|_2\le r,\ \|Z\|_{\rm op}\le s\},\quad D=\operatorname{RowClip}_r\!\left(\operatorname{Spectral}_s(\operatorname{Row}_r(M))\right)Kr,s​={Z:imax​∥Zi,:​∥2​≤r, ∥Z∥op​≤s},D=RowClipr​(Spectrals​(Rowr​(M)))
Evidence boundary

Language experiments mainly use a four-layer, width-128 model on Tiny Shakespeare. No extra overhead is not demonstrated large-scale end-to-end acceleration. Fixed-point/truncation theory does not directly establish global convergence of nonconvex LLM training with Sparrow. [1]

Ensembles cannot see information all members miss

Scaling Laws of Ensemble Uncertainty in Language Models ↗
Ravanbakhsh, Siamak · Nitchi, David · Ngo, Khang
v1: November 24, 2026 ET; November 25 announcement

Let pₓ be the true next-token distribution and qₛ the S members. The paper separates irreducible entropy, reducible error and ensemble-recoverable disagreement through log loss. Agreement does not exhaust epistemic uncertainty. [2]

The paper’s identity needs no model calibration; identifying reducible error with a particular information-theoretic epistemic quantity requires additional calibration conditions.
L(q)=EXH(pX)+EXDKL(pX∥qX),Lˉ=1S∑sL(qs)L(q)=\mathbb E_XH(p_X)+\mathbb E_XD_{\rm KL}(p_X\|q_X),\qquad \bar L=\frac1S\sum_sL(q_s)L(q)=EX​H(pX​)+EX​DKL​(pX​∥qX​),Lˉ=S1​s∑​L(qs​)
The paper’s geometric pooling averages log probabilities and renormalizes. ρG is recovered loss, not all reducible error.
qG(y∣x)=exp⁡{S−1∑slog⁡qs(y∣x)}Zx,ρG=Lˉ−L(qG)=1S∑sEXDKL(qG∥qs)q_G(y\mid x)=\frac{\exp\{S^{-1}\sum_s\log q_s(y\mid x)\}}{Z_x},\quad\rho_G=\bar L-L(q_G)=\frac1S\sum_s\mathbb E_XD_{\rm KL}(q_G\|q_s)qG​(y∣x)=Zx​exp{S−1∑s​logqs​(y∣x)}​,ρG​=Lˉ−L(qG​)=S1​s∑​EX​DKL​(qG​∥qs​)
The paper’s visible/invisible decomposition. Their sum is mean-member reducible log loss; shared representation limits can remain in the second term.
Lˉ−H(Y∣X)=ρG+[L(qG)−H(Y∣X)]\bar L-H(Y\mid X)=\rho_G+\bigl[L(q_G)-H(Y\mid X)\bigr]Lˉ−H(Y∣X)=ρG​+[L(qG​)−H(Y∣X)]

On recent text, public-model fits give geometric-spread parameter exponents about 0.118–0.200 versus loss exponents about 0.191–0.313 under a shared-floor assumption. Later branching further reduces diversity. Different decay rates support growing competitiveness with scale, not compute optimality for arbitrary hardware/training budgets. [2]

Evidence boundary

Floor estimates, model families, data and pooling affect fits. The S/(S−1) population correction relies on nearby-prediction and independence approximations; it is not an exactly unbiased correction for members sharing training history. [2]

TipSweep: precision changes the depth-transfer exponent

Precision-Aware Learning Rate Transfer Across Block Count in LLM Post-Training ↗
Chen, Nan · Villar, Soledad · Hayou, Soufiane
v1: 2026-11-23 ET; delayed addition

Delayed addition: tune only the final k₀ blocks, then transfer to k trainable blocks. Rates scale approximately as k⁻¹ with FP32 master weights and k⁻¹ᐟ² under direct BF16 updates. Read the PDF abstract and introduction; rounding-model assumptions were not exhaustively audited. There is no single rule across arbitrary precision formats. [3]

One spectral scale, different risk exponents

Metric-Dependent Scaling Laws for Sketched Multiclass Softmax Regression ↗
Chen, Ziyan · Zheng, Linhe · Liu, Peilin · Zhou, Zhongzhu · Zhou, Ding-Xuan
v1: 2026-11-24 ET; November 25 announcement

In finite-sample-reuse sketched multiclass softmax, cross-entropy excess and excess 0–1 risk share a spectral scale; teacher–student decision disagreement takes its square root. Gaussian teacher–student, spectral and decision-nondegeneracy assumptions matter. Based on abstract and introduction. [4]

Proof-RL replication: rubric scores versus full-proof rates

FineProofEnvs: Better Math Proofs from a 2B Model with RL ↗
Adithya S K
First published November 24, 2026 (site date)

Qwen3.5-2B trains directly from base with RL on 1,024 problems for 500 steps. Four samples on each of 64 selected held-out problems raise normalized rubric means from 0.217 to 0.407 and full-mark attempts from 12.1% to 25.4%. These are LLM-judge scores, not Lean verification; evaluation problems were filtered for reference-proof gradeability. [15]

From zero training error to symmetric classification geometry

Prevalence of neural collapse during the terminal phase of deep learning training ↗
Vardan Papyan · X. Y. Han · David L. Donoho
PNAS 2020; first posted August 18, 2020; read v2: August 21, 2020
1,217 citations in the queried OpenAlex record (November 25, 2026) ↗

After training classification error reaches zero, cross-entropy training still changes penultimate representations. Papyan, Han and Donoho (PNAS 2020) organize terminal-phase geometry as Neural Collapse. It is first a collection of empirical phenomena, not a global SGD-convergence theorem for arbitrary networks. [16]

Notation restatement of the paper’s balanced-class setting: C classes and n training features h per class. W measures within-class scatter and B between-class scatter.
μc=1n∑ihic,μG=1C∑cμc,ΣW=1Cn∑c,i(hic−μc)(hic−μc)⊤,ΣB=1C∑c(μc−μG)(μc−μG)⊤\mu_c=\frac1n\sum_i h_{ic},\quad\mu_G=\frac1C\sum_c\mu_c,\quad\Sigma_W=\frac1{Cn}\sum_{c,i}(h_{ic}-\mu_c)(h_{ic}-\mu_c)^\top,\quad\Sigma_B=\frac1C\sum_c(\mu_c-\mu_G)(\mu_c-\mu_G)^\topμc​=n1​i∑​hic​,μG​=C1​c∑​μc​,ΣW​=Cn1​c,i∑​(hic​−μc​)(hic​−μc​)⊤,ΣB​=C1​c∑​(μc​−μG​)(μc​−μG​)⊤
A normalized NC1 diagnostic: within-class variability shrinks relative to between-class geometry; feature norms need not vanish.
N1=1Ctr⁡(ΣWΣB†)⟶0\mathcal N_1=\frac1C\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_W\Sigma_B^\dagger)\longrightarrow0N1​=C1​tr(ΣW​ΣB†​)⟶0
NC2: centered means approach equal norms and pairwise cosine −1/(C−1), a simplex ETF. Feature dimension must accommodate C−1 dimensions.
vc=μc−μG∥μc−μG∥,V=[v1,…,vC],V⊤V⟶CC−1 ⁣(I−1C11⊤)v_c=\frac{\mu_c-\mu_G}{\|\mu_c-\mu_G\|},\quad V=[v_1,\ldots,v_C],\quad V^\top V\longrightarrow\frac{C}{C-1}\!\left(I-\frac1C\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\right)vc​=∥μc​−μG​∥μc​−μG​​,V=[v1​,…,vC​],V⊤V⟶C−1C​(I−C1​11⊤)
NC3 is classifier–centered-mean self-duality. The right identity is an algebraic derivation under ideal equal norms and common bias, explaining NC4’s nearest-class-center rule; it does not guarantee accuracy on arbitrary unseen samples.
wc∥wc∥⟶vc,arg⁡max⁡c⟨μc−μG,h−μG⟩=arg⁡min⁡c∥h−μc∥2\frac{w_c}{\|w_c\|}\longrightarrow v_c,\qquad \arg\max_c\langle\mu_c-\mu_G,h-\mu_G\rangle=\arg\min_c\|h-\mu_c\|^2∥wc​∥wc​​⟶vc​,argcmax​⟨μc​−μG​,h−μG​⟩=argcmin​∥h−μc​∥2

The paper tracks post-zero-error training geometry across datasets, networks and loss settings. Within-class collapse, simplex ETF geometry, self-duality and NCC decisions should be tested separately; one nearly satisfied diagnostic does not imply all four. [16]

Evidence boundary

Read definitions, empirical evidence and relevant arguments; experiments not reproduced. Balanced classes, sufficient feature dimension and terminal training regime matter. Regular training geometry does not automatically explain test generalization or tail-class performance. [16]
