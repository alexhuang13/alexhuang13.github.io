BReD: how momentum remembers small bias

BReD: Block Replay Dithering for Stable Low-Bit EMA Optimizer States ↗
Zhan, Heshen · Huang, Youhan · Peng, Yunke · Wang, Yao · Kong, Linghui · Zhu, Ziwei · Han, Qingyu · Wang, Yaoyuan · Chen, Congliang · Sun, Ruoyu
v1: November 25, 2026 ET; November 26 announcement

Let s be the full-precision EMA driven by the same input sequence, sᵈᑫ the dequantized state and e=sᵈᑫ−s. Each write-back injects ε. The paper first isolates storage error; it does not assume that gradients along two parameter trajectories coincide. [1]

Paper theorem: ξ is temporally independent, zero mean, with second moment σ²; the initial state has finite second moment and is independent. β∈(0,1). Bias is amplified faster as β approaches one.
et+1=βet+ϵt+1,ϵt=b+ξt,lim⁡t→∞E∥et∥F2=∥b∥F2(1−β)2+σ21−β2e_{t+1}=\beta e_t+\epsilon_{t+1},\quad\epsilon_t=b+\xi_t,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb E\|e_t\|_F^2=\frac{\|b\|_F^2}{(1-\beta)^2}+\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\beta^2}et+1​=βet​+ϵt+1​,ϵt​=b+ξt​,t→∞lim​E∥et​∥F2​=(1−β)2∥b∥F2​​+1−β2σ2​
The paper’s local non-clipped interval model: p₀ and p₀+Δ are adjacent grid points. Subtractive dithering is unbiased, but does not have smaller variance than stochastic rounding at every x.
α=x−p0Δ,u∼U(0,1),x^=p0+Δ(1{α+u≥1}−u+12),Var⁡(x^−x∣x)=Δ212\alpha=\frac{x-p_0}{\Delta},\quad u\sim U(0,1),\quad \widehat x=p_0+\Delta\left(\mathbf1\{\alpha+u\ge1\}-u+\frac12\right),\quad\operatorname{Var}(\widehat x-x\mid x)=\frac{\Delta^2}{12}α=Δx−p0​​,u∼U(0,1),x=p0​+Δ(1{α+u≥1}−u+21​),Var(x−x∣x)=12Δ2​

BReD replays the same random value keyed by state, step and block. Sharing dither within a block reduces generation cost and introduces correlation. It compresses persistent states while preserving optimizer computation and hyperparameters. [1]

Evidence boundary

The table summarizes evaluated settings, not guarantees for every task. Embedding states remain full precision during pretraining. The EMA theorem is not a full Muon convergence proof with clipping, correlated errors and closed-loop gradients. [1]

LoopTitan: make weight sharing the final ablation

LoopTitan: What Does Parameter Revisit Buy a Mixture-of-Experts? ↗
Tian, Zichen · Lu, Yudong · Wu, Xuansheng · Zou, Na · Hu, Xia · Wang, Guanchu · Sun, Qianru
v1: November 25, 2026 ET; November 26 announcement

The main setting uses 1.3B stored and 0.2B active parameters with 5B DCLM tokens. Network shape, expert layout and residual scale are changed before sharing middle-layer parameters. The non-revisit control spreads experts over more independent layers. [2]

Paper Proposition 1: a is a residual multiplier and W a branch-ending output projection. It requires the same batches and no gradient-norm clipping; Muon must not hit its normalization floor, and AdamW additionally needs ε→ε/a.
(a;W0,ηt,λ) ⟺ (1;aW0,aηt,λ/a),Vt=aWt(a;W_0,\eta_t,\lambda)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (1;aW_0,a\eta_t,\lambda/a),\qquad V_t=aW_t(a;W0​,ηt​,λ) ⟺ (1;aW0​,aηt​,λ/a),Vt​=aWt​

Residual scaling therefore changes initialization, effective learning rate and weight decay together, so its benefit cannot be assigned to recurrence alone. The non-shared control retains the looped recipe’s gain at matched budgets, but has six extra attention blocks and 1.1% more parameters; matching is not exact. [2]

An extra visit does help when compute increases at fixed parameters. Under Muon, a deeper standard MoE at equal parameters and compute recovers 90.2% of that gain. This is recovery of a local test-loss gap, not a 90.2% improvement in final capability or a training-cost reduction. [2]

Evidence boundary

The main metric is final test bits per byte. Some comparisons use three seeds and others one. Calling differences below two standard errors a match does not prove that sharing is useless at every scale. [2]

Parameter allocation in looped MoE

Scaling Looped MoE Language Models under Matched Budgets: Information Flow and Parameter Allocation ↗
Bai, Shipeng · Qin, Jiayu · Liu, Hong · Wang, Xili · Tan, Jianchao · Li, Jiacheng · Zhang, He · Lu, Yifan · Zhang, Jiaqi · Zhang, Kefeng · Xie, Yuchen · Cai, Xunliang
v1: November 25, 2026 ET; November 26 announcement

At matched parameters/FLOPs, gated recurrence and cross-stage residuals use a one-pass Core of 25%–35% of unrolled depth and reallocate saved parameters to experts. After 1T tokens, 69B MLA and 51B KDA gain 1.16 and 2.29 points averaged over four benchmarks. This tests a design package, unlike LoopTitan’s isolated sharing question. [3]

Drift-triggered polar restoration

Amortizing Exact Orthogonality: Drift-Triggered Restoration for Orthogonal Fine-Tuning ↗
Zhao, Lanxin · Li, Xiaoyu · Shi, Dai · Gao, Junbin · Han, Andi
v1: November 24, 2026 ET; November 26 announcement

RTOFT uses I+S with skew-symmetric S; its orthogonality defect is exactly ‖S‖². Threshold-triggered restoration occurs before the next forward pass. The τ² guarantee requires per-step detection and restoration order, not once-per-epoch repair. This is orthogonal fine-tuning, not a new global Muon theory. [6]

Kim, Pureum · Ryu, Younggeon · Lee, Dongyoon · Lee, Hae Beom · Jin, Kyong Hwan

Loop Behind: Rethinking Where Extra Loops Pay Off in Looped Language Models

Loop Behind: Rethinking Where Extra Loops Pay Off in Looped Language Models ↗
Xijie Gong · Tonghan Wang
First posted November 24, 2026; delayed inclusion

On Dory-4B, most extra-loop gain comes from deeper KV of preceding tokens rather than more current-token computation before readout. Across 12 benchmarks, deeper KV recovers 6.8 of a 7.2-point gap. Loop-Behind overlaps remaining loops with later tokens. That fraction concerns a local accuracy gap, not overall performance. [11]

Two teacher steps to one student step: invert DDIM

Progressive Distillation for Fast Sampling of Diffusion Models ↗
Tim Salimans · Jonathan Ho
ICLR 2022; first posted February 1, 2022; read v2: June 7, 2022
OpenAlex: 3,057 citations (November 26, 2026) ↗

DDIM (2020) makes generation deterministic, but reducing steps directly increases discretization error. Salimans and Ho (ICLR 2022) instead train a student to match the endpoint of two teacher steps, repeatedly halving the step count. [14]

Paper setting and DDIM step: s<t, ε is standard Gaussian and log-SNR decreases with t. The second argument of D is the denoising prediction.
zt=αtx+σtϵ,αt2+σt2=1,Dt→s(z;x^)=σsσtz+(αs−σsσtαt)x^z_t=\alpha_t x+\sigma_t\epsilon,\quad\alpha_t^2+\sigma_t^2=1,\quad D_{t\to s}(z;\widehat x)=\frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_t}z+\left(\alpha_s-\frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_t}\alpha_t\right)\widehat xzt​=αt​x+σt​ϵ,αt2​+σt2​=1,Dt→s​(z;x)=σt​σs​​z+(αs​−σt​σs​​αt​)x
Let zᵤ be the intermediate teacher state. The frozen teacher supplies a deterministic endpoint for a given zₜ; the target is not simply the original x.
zsteach=Du→s ⁣(Dt→u(zt;x^η(zt));x^η(zu)),s<u<tz_s^{\rm teach}=D_{u\to s}\!\left(D_{t\to u}(z_t;\widehat x_\eta(z_t));\widehat x_\eta(z_u)\right),\quad s<u<tzsteach​=Du→s​(Dt→u​(zt​;xη​(zt​));xη​(zu​)),s<u<t
Inverting one DDIM step gives the paper’s target. Two teacher steps span one student step. Each student is initialized from the teacher and becomes the next stage’s teacher.
x~=zsteach−(σs/σt)ztαs−(σs/σt)αt,min⁡θE w(λt)∥x^θ(zt)−x~∥2\widetilde x=\frac{z_s^{\rm teach}-(\sigma_s/\sigma_t)z_t}{\alpha_s-(\sigma_s/\sigma_t)\alpha_t},\qquad\min_\theta\mathbb E\,w(\lambda_t)\|\widehat x_\theta(z_t)-\widetilde x\|^2x=αs​−(σs​/σt​)αt​zsteach​−(σs​/σt​)zt​​,θmin​Ew(λt​)∥xθ​(zt​)−x∥2
The paper’s v parameterization avoids dividing by α as α→0, unlike ε prediction. Appropriate loss weighting is still required; parameterization alone does not eliminate distillation error.
v=αtϵ−σtx,x^=αtzt−σtv^,x^ϵ=zt−σtϵ^αtv=\alpha_t\epsilon-\sigma_t x,\quad\widehat x=\alpha_t z_t-\sigma_t\widehat v,\qquad \widehat x_{\epsilon}=\frac{z_t-\sigma_t\widehat\epsilon}{\alpha_t}v=αt​ϵ−σt​x,x=αt​zt​−σt​v,xϵ​=αt​zt​−σt​ϵ​

The paper reports roughly 3.0 FID on CIFAR-10 with four steps under its model, training and evaluation protocol. Teacher endpoint errors can propagate across stages; one-step approximation does not guarantee preservation of every distributional detail. [14]
